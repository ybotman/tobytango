# Carlos Gardel

El Zorzal Criollo — the Songbird of Buenos Aires. Carlos Gardel is tango. The most famous tango singer of all time, he created the tango-canción with "Mi Noche Triste" (1917), sold 70,000 records in three months during his 1928 Paris tour, and starred in Paramount films that brought tango to global audiences. When he died in a plane crash at the height of his fame, tens of thousands mourned in Buenos Aires. Eight decades later, Argentines still say "Gardel sings better every day." His tomb remains a pilgrimage site.

---

## Biography

### Birth and Controversy

Carlos Gardel (born Charles Romuald Gardès) was born on December 11, 1890, in Toulouse, France — though his birthplace remains contentious. In 1931, Gardel wrote in a witnessed document: "I am French, born in Toulouse, 11 December 1890, son of Berthe Gardès." Yet he sometimes claimed Tacuarembó, Uruguay, saying in 1935: "My heart is Argentine, but my soul is Uruguayan."

His birth certificate was confirmed in Toulouse in 2012. The controversy arose from false papers filed after 1920 when he applied for citizenship.

### Buenos Aires Childhood

Gardel arrived in Buenos Aires by age six. He grew up in the Abasto neighborhood, starting his career singing at parties and local events.

### Early Career

He began singing in bars and at private parties, first with Francisco Martino, then in a trio with Martino and José Razzano. His first formal acting roles were at the Nacional Corrientes Theatre.

### Creating Tango-Canción (1917)

**Gardel created the tango-canción in 1917** with his recording of Pascual Contursi and Samuel Castriota's "Mi Noche Triste." The recording sold 10,000 copies and was a hit throughout Latin America. Before this, tango was primarily instrumental; Gardel made the sung tango an art form.

### Partnership with José Razzano

Gardel formed a long-standing musical partnership with Don José Razzano. Together they toured Argentina, Latin America, and Spain with theatre companies.

### International Stardom (1920s–30s)

Gardel toured extensively: Argentina, Uruguay, Chile, Brazil, Puerto Rico, Venezuela, Colombia, Paris, New York, Barcelona, and Madrid. His 1928 Paris visit sold 70,000 records in the first three months.

### Film Career

As tango's popularity grew, Gardel made films for Paramount Pictures — sentimental films that showcased his tremendous vocal talents:

### Death in Medellín (1935)

On June 24, 1935, Gardel was killed in a plane crash near Medellín, Colombia, while touring with his entourage and collaborators. He was 44 years old, at the absolute height of his career.

His Buenos Aires funeral procession was witnessed by tens of thousands. Like Rudolf Valentino, his tomb became a pilgrimage destination — and remains so today.

## Legacy

### "Gardel Sings Better Every Day"

A popular Argentine saying testifies to his enduring popularity. Though he died in 1935, his influence only grows.

### "Twenty Years Is Nothing"

The phrase "Veinte años no es nada" from his song "Volver" (1934) has become a common expression in Spanish.

### Pilgrimage Site

His tomb in Buenos Aires's Chacarita Cemetery remains a pilgrimage destination, with fresh flowers and lit cigarettes left daily by devotees.

### The Soul of Tango

> "For many, Gardel embodies the soul of the tango style."

### The Tragic Hero

Dying at the height of his career, Gardel became an archetypal tragic hero mourned throughout Latin America — forever young, forever perfect, forever singing.
