# Francisco Canaro

Pirincho — the most prolific tango recording artist in history. Francisco Canaro made his first violin from an oil can and went on to record over 3,500 pieces of music. He founded SADAIC (Argentina's copyright society), toured Europe for a decade, and shaped tango from the Guardia Vieja through the Golden Age. His 1956 memoir *Mis 50 años con el tango* documented a half-century at the center of tango's evolution.

---

## Biography

### From Uruguay to Buenos Aires

Born November 26, 1888 in San José de Mayo, Uruguay, to Italian immigrant parents, Canaro moved to Buenos Aires before age 10. The family was poor, and the young Francisco worked in a factory.

### The Oil Can Violin

In a legendary origin story, Canaro fashioned his first violin from an empty oil can. His natural talent overcame his modest beginnings.

### Entry into Tango (1908)

Canaro's introduction to tango came through orquesta típica leader Vicente Greco in 1908. By 1912, he had composed "Pinta brava" ("Fierce Look"), establishing himself as both performer and composer.

### Paris Decade (1925-1935)

In 1925, Canaro brought his orchestra to Paris and found tremendous success among European audiences. He remained in Europe for a decade, spreading tango internationally during its golden era abroad.

### Founding SADAIC (1935)

Beginning in 1918, Canaro fought for copyright protection for musicians — a right not recognized at the time. His persistent advocacy culminated in the founding of **SADAIC** (Argentine Society of Authors and Composers of Music) in 1935. The organization's building at 1547 Lavalle Street in the Tribunales neighborhood was built on land Canaro himself acquired.

### Recording Legacy

Canaro became the most recorded tango artist in history. Estimates vary:
- Over 7,000 recordings (some sources)
- 3,792 recordings (other sources)
- Over 3,500 pieces of music (conservative estimate)

Whatever the exact count, his prolific output is unmatched.

### Memoir and Retirement

In 1956, Canaro published *Mis 50 años con el tango* (My Fifty Years with the Tango), documenting his extraordinary career. He later developed Paget's disease and was forced to retire. He died December 14, 1964.

## Musical Eras

Canaro's career spanned three tango eras:

### Guardia Vieja (Old Guard)
His earliest work connected to tango's origins and Vicente Greco's traditional style.

### Guardia Nueva (New Guard)
He evolved with the changing times, adapting to more sophisticated arrangements.

### Época de Oro (Golden Age)
He continued recording through tango's peak years, remaining relevant across generations.

---

## Legacy

### Copyright Pioneer

Canaro's greatest off-stage contribution was establishing legal protections for musicians. SADAIC continues today as the essential organization protecting Argentine composers' rights.

### Recording Pioneer

His sheer output documented tango's evolution across five decades. His recordings serve as an archive of tango's development.

### International Ambassador

His decade in Europe helped establish tango as a global phenomenon, not just an Argentine tradition.
