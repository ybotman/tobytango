# Susana Miller

The woman who named it. Susana Miller coined the term "milonguero style" in the mid-1990s, systematized its teaching with Cacho Dante and Tete Rusconi, and brought it to the world. She learned from the old milongueros when few teachers existed, then figured out how to transmit their feel, rhythm, and choreography to a new generation. The Buenos Aires newspaper Clarín claimed she "probably put more people on the dance floor than any other single teacher." She founded La Academia de Tango Milonguero and owns El Beso, one of Buenos Aires' most beloved milongas.

---

## Biography

### Buenos Aires Origins

Susana Miller was born and raised in Buenos Aires, immersed in tango culture from childhood through family gatherings and milongas.

### Learning from the Last Milongueros (1980s)

When Miller began to learn tango in the mid-1980s, there were few tango teachers. Her first teachers were the old milongueros — veterans of the Golden Age who had danced through the 1940s and 1950s.

### Key Mentors

- **El Gallego Manolo** — Most influential mentor; emphasized close embrace and intimate partner connection. His philosophy profoundly shaped Miller's teaching approach.
- **Pupy Castello** — Known for precise technique and musicality

### Naming Milonguero Style (mid-1990s)

Miller introduced the term "Milonguero Style" in the mid-1990s while developing a new didactic system for teaching tango. She was assisted by:

- **Cacho Dante** — Milonguero who taught with Susana for several years before teaching independently; joined her on a world tour that would last the rest of his life
- **Pedro "Tete" Rusconi** — Vals master, Guardian generation
- Other veteran milongueros

Together, they developed systematic methods to teach what had previously been transmitted only through osmosis in the milongas.

### La Academia de Tango Milonguero

Miller founded La Academia de Tango Milonguero in Buenos Aires — a cornerstone institution for tango education offering classes, workshops, and seminars emphasizing traditional milonguero style. The academy has trained countless dancers and teachers.

### El Beso

She owns El Beso, one of Buenos Aires' most popular milongas, providing a home for the style she champions.

### The Encuentro de Tango Milonguero

The Academia organizes the annual Encuentro de Tango Milonguero festival, pioneering the encuentro format that would spread worldwide — intimate gatherings focused on social dancing rather than performances.

## Teaching Philosophy

### Four Core Principles

1. **Connection over choreography** — Prioritizing partner unity above all
2. **Emotional expression** — Conveying feelings through movement
3. **Musicality** — Deep listening and interpretation of the music
4. **Respect for tradition** — Grounding in authentic techniques

### The Embrace First

Miller emphasizes fundamentals — walking, posture, and the embrace — before complex figures. Her approach focuses on "the embrace, the heart-to-heart connection" that defines milonguero style.

### Deciphering the Masters

She researched and studied each older milonguero's unique way of dancing, deciphering their distinct vocabularies and choreographic combinations, then found ways to transmit these to students.

## Recognition

### Clarín's Four Most Important

The major Buenos Aires daily paper Clarín named her one of the four most important influences on contemporary tango, alongside:
- Miguel Ángel Zotto
- Gustavo Naveira
- Gerardo Portalea

### Global Influence

Her work has influenced tango not only in Buenos Aires but throughout the world. She conducts international workshops across North America, Europe, Asia, and Australia.

### Publications

Miller has published instructional materials including books and DVDs on tango milonguero.

## Legacy

### Naming and Systematizing

By coining "milonguero style" and developing systematic teaching methods, Miller gave the world a vocabulary and framework for understanding this intimate, close-embrace approach to tango.

### The Encuentro Format

Her annual Encuentro de Tango Milonguero pioneered the encuentro format — now found worldwide — that emphasizes social dancing, codes of the milonga, and community over performance.

### Preserving the Feel

While others documented steps and figures, Miller focused on transmitting the feel, rhythm, and emotional content of the old milongueros' dancing — the intangible qualities that make tango come alive.
