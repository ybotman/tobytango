# Gerardo Portalea

The patio dancer. Gerardo Portalea was a legendary Villa Urquiza salon dancer — slow, elegant, deliberate. A serious, modest, reserved man who worked as a telephone company employee for 25 years, he never sought to professionalize himself, yet became a teacher to many of the most prominent tango dancers of our time. After a 17-year hiatus, he returned with unwavering loyalty to Villa Urquiza's dance halls, calling himself simply a "bailarín de patio" (patio dancer).

---

## Biography

### Golden Age Origins

Born in 1929, Gerardo Portalea began dancing in the 1940s at Club Sin Rumbo — the historic venue in Villa Urquiza that shaped the neighborhood's distinctive style.

### The Milonguita Connection

Portalea inherited his style from Luis "Milonguita" Lemos, who left his legacy at historic family clubs like Sin Rumbo. This lineage connects Portalea directly to the creation of Villa Urquiza style.

### Day Job

For 25 years, Portalea worked as a telephone company employee. He never sought to make tango his profession.

### 17-Year Absence

At some point, Portalea stayed away from dancing for seventeen years. When he returned, he maintained unwavering loyalty to Villa Urquiza's dance halls.

### International Teaching

Despite his modest self-image, Portalea traveled to Chile, France, Germany, and Norway to teach and give demonstrations. He occasionally appeared in films and shows.

### Danza Maligna (2003)

His most significant stage appearance was in "Danza Maligna," a show by Silvana Grill and Fabián Luca that united professional dancers with authentic milongueros. It was presented at the Théâtre de Chaillot in Paris.

## Style

### Slow and Deliberate

Portalea was famous for his slow, elegant, deliberate style — the hallmarks of classic Villa Urquiza tango.

### Tango Estilo del Barrio

His dance was representative of Tango Estilo del Barrio — elegant, walked, sober yet rich, deeply musical. Often called "tango-salon."

### "Bailarín de Patio"

He described himself as a "patio dancer" — emphasizing his roots in social dancing rather than stage performance.

---

## Students & Influence

Despite never professionalizing, Portalea became a teacher to many prominent tango dancers. Videos of his demonstrations at milongas remain examples of classic Villa Urquiza style.
