# Carlos Di Sarli — "El Señor del Tango"

The Lord of Tango created the most elegant, walking music in the genre's history. Carlos Di Sarli's piano-driven sound — melodic, refined, perfectly balanced between danceability and sophistication — remains the gold standard for milonga DJs worldwide. His distinctive dark glasses concealed an eye lost in childhood, but also symbolized his artistic mystery. When musicians asked him to explain his style, he simply replied: "You follow the sheets. The style, I make it myself."

---

## Biography

### Childhood in Bahía Blanca

Carlos Di Sarli was born Cayetano Di Sarli on January 7, 1903, in Bahía Blanca, a port city in southern Argentina. He was the eighth child of Italian immigrant Miguel Di Sarli, owner of a gunsmith store, and Serafina Russomano, daughter of tenor singer Tito Russomano. He later changed his name to Carlos.

### The Eye Injury

At age 13, Carlos lost an eye in a firearms accident at his father's store. He wore dark glasses for the rest of his life to cover the injury, becoming one of his most recognizable features.

### Classical Training

Carlos received training in classical music at the conservatory where his brother taught. With help from composer Alberico Spatola, he was able to join the band of Anselmo Aieta, beginning his professional career.

### Move to Buenos Aires (1923)

Di Sarli moved to Buenos Aires in 1923, working as a pianist in various groups. In 1926, he joined Osvaldo Fresedo's orchestra.

### First Orchestra and the Germinal Incident

In late 1927, Di Sarli established his first sextet. But his career in Buenos Aires was nearly derailed by pride: when an owner of the Germinal Café told him to "stop the stupid stardom and take those glasses off," Di Sarli was so offended that he turned around and left for Bahía Blanca — and his entire orchestra followed him out of solidarity.

### Recording Career Begins (1928)

On November 26, 1928, Di Sarli signed with RCA-Victor, recording 48 pieces through 1931. A period of organizational changes followed from 1932–1938.

### The Glory Years (1939–1949)

In January 1939, Di Sarli debuted his reorganized orchestra on Radio El Mundo. This began his "period of glory" — the decade when his elegant, walking style defined Golden Age tango for dancers.

### Later Recordings

- **1951–1953**: Recorded 84 releases for Music Hall
- **1954–1958**: Returned to Victor Records
- **1958**: Final 14 releases on Philips label

### Final Performance (1959)

Di Sarli, already seriously ill, gave his last performance on March 8, 1959, at the club Podestá de Lanús, finishing with "Bahía Blanca" — his own composition dedicated to his hometown.

### Death and Tribute

Carlos Di Sarli died on January 12, 1960, in Buenos Aires. At his passing, Aníbal Troilo said:

> "The blind took the secret to the grave."

This tribute acknowledged how Di Sarli kept his artistic innovations closely guarded, never fully explaining how he achieved his distinctive sound.

## The Orchestra

### Notable Singers

Di Sarli worked with many of the era's finest vocalists:

- **Roberto Rufino** — powerful voice of the early 1940s
- **Oscar Serpa** — lyrical interpretation
- **Mario Pomar** — smooth delivery
- **Jorge Durán** — emotional depth
- **Argentino Ledesma** — distinctive phrasing

### The Di Sarli Sound

Di Sarli combined rhythmic cadence with harmonic structure that was "apparently simple, but full of nuances." He avoided both traditional orchestral styles and avant-garde movements, establishing his own signature throughout his career.

## Style & Philosophy

### "The Style, I Make It Myself"

In 1956, new orchestra members asked: "Maestro, could you tell us something about the style?"

Di Sarli's response became legendary:

> "Gentlemen, you just follow the sheets and play what's written. The style, I make it myself."

### Elegance and Balance

Di Sarli's style was defined by:

- **Piano-driven sound** — the piano as melodic leader
- **Elegant phrasing** — refined without being precious
- **Perfect danceability** — "walking" music for close embrace
- **Harmonic subtlety** — simple on the surface, complex beneath
- **Balance** — neither too traditional nor too avant-garde

### The Secret

Di Sarli never fully revealed how he achieved his distinctive sound. He improvised at the piano, adding nuances that weren't in the written arrangements. When he died, Troilo said "the blind took the secret to the grave."

## The Dark Glasses

Di Sarli's signature dark glasses, worn to conceal his blind eye, became part of his mystique. The Germinal Café incident showed his fierce pride — he would rather abandon Buenos Aires than remove them.
