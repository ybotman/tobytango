# Fabián Salas

Argentine tango dancer, teacher, choreographer, and organizer — co-founder of tango nuevo alongside Gustavo Naveira and creator of CITA (Congreso Internacional de Tango Argentino), the world's longest-running tango festival. A trained lawyer who abandoned law for tango, Salas pioneered the analytical teaching methodology that revolutionized how tango is understood and transmitted worldwide. With his wife and partner Lola Díaz, he continues to teach, perform, and organize major tango events from Buenos Aires.

---

## Biography

### Early Life and Education

Fabián Salas was born in 1963 in La Plata, capital of Buenos Aires province. He was educated in both Argentina and the United States and trained as a lawyer. In 1988, he moved to the city of Buenos Aires — ostensibly to practice law, but the pull of tango proved stronger.

### The Turn to Tango (1988–1992)

By 1992, Salas had begun touring the world as a tango teacher, leaving his legal career behind. What drove him was a sense of urgency: the old milongueros who carried tango's embodied knowledge were dying, and their wisdom was not being systematically transmitted.

As Salas later recalled: "We are on our own."

### The Investigation Group (1995)

In 1995, Salas co-founded what would become known as the Tango Investigation Group with Gustavo Naveira. Together, they applied principles of dance kinesiology from modern dance to analyze the physics of tango movement.

Their revolutionary insight: instead of teaching sequences and choreography, they deconstructed tango to its fundamental building blocks. From these elements, dancers could create their own movement vocabulary. This approach became known as "tango nuevo" — though both Salas and Naveira have always been critical of the term as a style label.

> "We found a structure — something that is fundamental for every type of tango that you will do."

### Film Career (1996–1997)

In 1996, Salas participated in the film *Evita*. A year later, he was one of the three principal dancers in Sally Potter's award-winning film *The Tango Lesson* (1997), alongside Gustavo Naveira and Pablo Verón. In the film's iconic Confitería Ideal scene, Salas and Naveira appear as themselves — Potter's tango teachers in Buenos Aires.

### Founding CITA (1999)

In 1999, Salas founded the Congreso Internacional de Tango Argentino (CITA) in Buenos Aires. Held every March since then, CITA is recognized as the world's most important and longest-running annual tango festival, gathering practitioners, teachers, and artists representing the full diversity of Argentine tango.

CITA features:
- 90+ classes per week
- 50+ performances
- 30+ couples of world-acclaimed master teachers

The festival was awarded "Best Festival" by Premios Tango in both 2018 and 2019.

### Partnership with Lola Díaz (2009–present)

Since 2009, Salas has been dancing, touring, and organizing with his wife Lola Díaz. Together they run CITA and have taught thousands of students worldwide — in Argentina, Brazil, the USA, Canada, Mexico, Australia, Japan, Taiwan, Hong Kong, and across Europe.

Lola Díaz is also the founder and CEO of Premios Tango and Floritango.

## Style & Philosophy

### The Analytical Method

Salas and Naveira pioneered a didactically-based teaching method — the first of its kind in tango. Rather than teaching steps, they taught structure.

> "In ballet and ballroom there is technique, there is a formal technique. In tango, there is nothing — just what the teacher tells you at this moment, all of them telling you different things."

### Colgadas

Salas is particularly known for his development of the colgada — a movement with no clear historical predecessor, making it one of the genuine innovations to emerge from the tango nuevo investigation. Colgadas are categorized as side, back, circular, and front variations.

### Teaching Philosophy

The Investigation Group's mission was preservation through understanding. As older milongueros died, Salas and his collaborators worked to decode how they moved — not to copy them, but to understand the underlying structure that made their movement possible.

From this work came "a shift in all styles of tango away from teaching *what* to dance toward teaching *how* to dance."
