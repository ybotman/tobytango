# Miguel Ángel Zotto

The face of tango. Miguel Ángel Zotto is tango royalty — grandson and son of dancers, raised in the milongas, chosen as one of the three greatest tango dancers of the century, named Academic by the National Tango Academy of Buenos Aires. With Milena Plebs, he created Tango x 2, toured *Tango Argentino* through 56 cities, and won the María Ruanova Award — Argentina's highest dance honor. Yet he maintains the humility of a milonguero, personally greeting ordinary dancers despite his celebrity. Clarín named him one of four most important influences on contemporary tango.

---

## Biography

### Family Roots

Zotto comes from a genuine "tango family" with popular roots. His grandfather was a dancer, and his father was both a dancer and actor. He grew up listening to tango and began dancing at home, visiting traditional venues as a teenager.

### The Bricklayer Who Danced

Before becoming a professional, Zotto worked as a bricklayer with his father and brother — and they danced tango together on the job site. This working-class background kept him grounded throughout his international career.

### Training with Los Dinzel (1975–1981)

At seventeen, Zotto began studying tango with several teachers. In 1979 he discovered Rodolfo Dinzel, and intensified his studies. He trained with Los Dinzel for six years, and later suggested Dinzel as instructor at Universidad de Belgrano.

### Milonga Years

He danced in the milongas of Buenos Aires for six years, absorbing the styles of different neighborhoods. His style reflects the *Urquiza* barrio style.

### Antonio Todaro

Zotto met his main mentor, Antonio Todaro, early on in the Flores neighborhood. Todaro was one of the most influential teachers of the revival era.

### Teaching at Universidad de Belgrano (1984)

In 1984, Zotto began teaching tango at the Universidad de Belgrano in Buenos Aires — one of the first academic tango programs.

### Partnership with Milena Plebs (1985–)

In 1985, Zotto partnered with Milena Plebs, a classically trained ballet dancer. They debuted together in *Jazmines*.

### Tango Argentino (1986)

In 1986, Zotto and Plebs joined the legendary *Tango Argentino* show produced by Segovia and Orezzoli — the production that sparked the worldwide tango renaissance. The show toured 56 cities in North America and Europe. They stayed with the show for more than four years.

### Creating Tango x 2 (1988)

In 1988, Zotto and Plebs created the **Tango x 2 Company**, where they performed as principal dancers while also serving as directors and choreographers. They premiered the first "Tango x 2" show in 1989.

## Philosophy

### Bridging Stage and Milonga

Zotto prioritizes maintaining tango conventions even on stage. He minimizes choreography and ensures he leads his partner, bridging the gap between performance and authentic milonga culture.

### Respect for Tradition

Despite international fame, Zotto maintains a dual reputation: he is respected by milongueros in Buenos Aires, not just international audiences. He demonstrates remarkable humility, personally greeting ordinary dancers despite his celebrity status.

### On the Future of Tango

While acknowledging that poor instruction exists, Zotto expressed faith that dedicated students will eventually discover authentic tango:

> The serious practitioners who dedicate 15-20 years to tango will eventually understand its true tradition.

### The Importance of Documentation

> "Our filming was important."

Zotto studied tango partly through filming, using small cameras to capture milongueros. He believes documentation preserves what might otherwise be lost.

### The Eight

Zotto's teaching emphasizes "the eight" (*el ocho*) as fundamental to tango movement. His extensive research led him to recognize this element's symbolic and practical importance in technique instruction.

## Teaching

### Universidad de Belgrano (1984)

One of the first to bring tango into an academic setting in Buenos Aires.

### International Teaching

In recent years, Zotto with his partner Daiana Guspero carries out intensive didactic activity in Italy and major European cities through seminars and workshops.

### Creative Control

Zotto works independently on all show aspects, including choreography, staging, and lighting design.
