# Mariano "Chicho" Frúmboli

**One of the three most influential living tango dancers**, a self-described "kamikaze" who helped shatter tango's formal vocabulary in the 1990s and has spent the decades since grappling publicly with the consequences. Born September 21, 1970, in Buenos Aires, he arrived at tango through rock drumming and theater, joined the legendary [Naveira](/people/gustavo-naveira)/[Salas](/people/fabian-salas) investigation group, and became the dancer [Gustavo Naveira](/people/gustavo-naveira) called "absolutely the best dancer I ever saw." Now in his mid-fifties and still touring intensively across Europe, he remains a polarizing and vital figure — a master improviser who rejects the "Tango Nuevo" label he helped create and advocates passionately for tango's essence over its spectacle.

## Biography

### Family and Musical Roots

Mariano Frúmboli was born on September 21, 1970, in Buenos Aires, Argentina. His father was a fine arts professor who studied guitar — giving young Chicho his first exposure to tango through music:

> "My father had an artistic side that was significant. He was a fine arts professor, he studied the guitar, and I believe that this had a lot to do with my own artistic development, and creativity. I also remember that when I was a child my father often listened to Piazzolla, and that was my first contact with the tango; with the music more than anything."

The specific Piazzolla pieces his father played were "Adiós Nonino" and "Libertango" — memories Chicho calls "imborrables" (unforgettable).

### Early Life

Chicho's first encounter with tango was not in a class — it was at **Salón La Argentina**, an old Buenos Aires dance hall that no longer exists, where he went on a whim with a girlfriend:

> "I went crazy with the faces, the bodies in movement as if they were glued to each other… the music."

But he did not pursue tango immediately. Two years passed before Victoria Vieyra, a classmate in his theater studies with Cristina Banegas, brought him back to tango through classes with Ricardo Barrios.

At age 13 (circa 1984), he received his first drum set and began formal music study at the David Lebón Music School in Buenos Aires. He played drums for **14 years** in rock bands before tango overtook his life. From 1992 to 1998, he simultaneously studied theater for eight years under Cristina Banegas.

**Personal details:** His football team is **Huracán**. His hobbies include making music on his PC and drawing. When asked about his first thought on waking: "I think about the time I have left to live and that I mustn't waste it." His future aspiration: "A good father."

### Conversion to Tango (1993)

In 1993, Chicho attended a tango class taught by Ricardo Barrios and Victoria Vieyra. The experience was transformative:

> "I was into rock-and-roll; I had long hair and played the drums. I hated tango, I didn't like it one bit. But when I went to take a class with Ricardo Barrios and Victoria Vieyra, I embraced my dance partner for the first time and I got goose bumps. I said, 'there's something going on here…' and I never stopped."

Victoria Vieyra introduced him to the Tango Investigation Group led by [Gustavo Naveira](/people/gustavo-naveira) and [Fabián Salas](/people/fabian-salas). Naveira later said:

> "I met Chicho through Victoria, his partner at the time. Victoria was a contemporary dancer, actress, and many other things, a great artist. And she was learning tango, and she met Chicho as a partner in her acting work… with only 6 months of study, he was amazing as a tango dancer."

> "Chicho was absolutely the best dancer I ever saw. He started working with us, with Fabián and me, and we said, 'Well, we have to get him to really join us in this work, or else we have to break his legs!' He did crazy things no one else could do!"

### Europe and Expansion (1998–present)

In October 1998, Chicho arrived in Europe with Victoria Vieyra. He settled first in France, then Belgium. His 2024–2025 touring schedule — heavy with European festivals — is consistent with a European home base.

## Style & Philosophy

### The Master of the Rebound

Chicho is known worldwide as "the master of the rebound" (*rebote*). His signature technique draws on the swing rhythms embedded in his rock-and-roll background:

> "Maybe I'm a 'kamikaze.' It's what provokes in me a sensation or emotion that makes me move… What motivates me is being on the edge, the limit, on the point of falling, and managing to escape from that."

### The 50/50 Partnership

Chicho pioneered a partnership dynamic where the follower co-creates the dance in real time:

> "I don't think that woman is going to occupy more or less space, if not that the couple takes on more strength and power when it is a couple, with an equality between the two, and today that is really a division of 50/50. This has to do with the way the man is marking in the moment, if she cannot feel comfortable dancing, then I cannot dance."

> "If I want to take the movement to create a sacada, I have to communicate to my partner in the gentlest way… I can't mark only with my hands, I have to do a completely corporal marking, or I propose something and she responds but she does it with another proposal and I then follow her."

### Musicality as Core Identity

> "I believe that my best qualities have to do with my musicality, and obviously my creativity within that realm. I began as a musician and I continue to be one. The music is what moves me on all levels, I need to feel it in order to be able to dance."

> "I know the musical phrase and how long it lasts, I know when it ends, and I'll prepare the movement on the fly so that it corresponds perfectly with the music… [Understanding music structure] is the foundation of everything, crucial."

### Adaptability

> "When I danced with Lucia I did things in one way, and when I began to dance with Eugenia she proposed a different way of dancing, and so my body had to adapt itself… the style is something you search for with your partner, and not something that you find separately."

### Tango as Universal

> "My idea, and that of Gustavo Naveira and Fabián Salas as well, was always that the tango needed to be popular in the world. Obviously, it is born from one country and has its origins, but it needs to be universal. It cannot belong only to Buenos Aires."

## The Complete Interview Record

### Interview #1: El Tangauta, April 2005

On Tango Nuevo as marketing:
> "It's a question I ask myself too… It's first and foremost a question of marketing… I feel close to the electronic orchestras in the way I dance. But beyond the fact that I know many musicians who play in those orchestras, I identify more with the music of [Troilo](/people/anibal-troilo), [Pugliese](/people/osvaldo-pugliese) and [D'Arienzo](/people/juan-darienzo)."

On tango's essence:
> "A very strong connection between the man and the woman who dance tango. Nowadays people learn two or three steps and go dancing… I really believe that dancing is thinking about how to embrace your partner, perceiving how she feels, how much she weighs, smelling her perfume, paying attention to how she takes your hand, if she trembles, if she sweats…"

On being rejected:
> "Of course I've been rejected. A few months ago, in a milonga in Paris, I invited a girl who was dancing with a contemporary style and she said: 'No, no thank you' and I walked away saying 'Ouch, that's tough!'"

### Interview #2: ATDRC, Buenos Aires, March 2008

On his weakness:
> "I believe my weakness has to do with my inhibition, I still don't feel that I have exploited and showed everything I have to show, almost as if I haven't been able to give myself completely as I would like to."

### Interview #3: El Tangauta #182, December 2009 — The Landmark Interview with Milena Plebs

**The confession:**
> "I couldn't really transmit what I learned. I was caught up in the madness of creativity, because I saw a new vein in the evolution of movement. I threw myself into it body and soul, and I lost the ability to transmit the essence of tango that I have inside me. For this reason, I think that these days there are many people who don't understand or don't know what the true essence of this dance is."

**The contradictory role:**
> "I know that my role is contradictory, because I also collaborated in the genesis of this new movement. At that time, I was tired of the strict milonguero codes that didn't correspond with my time and, in rebelling, I tried to make my way. Today, I have become a milonguero again (laughs); I am against people who don't do the cabeceo, who have no codes or respect."

**The embrace as essence:**
> "A few years ago I went to the 'La Trastienda' milonga organized by Horacio Godoy. I walked in and I saw you [Milena]. I wanted to dance with you but second-guessed myself… I remember we were talking, then we embraced each other and in that moment I felt 40 years of tango. In the embrace, do you understand? We hadn't taken a single step!… For me that was the most powerful moment of the tanda."

**On the milonga of Almagro:**
> "It took me five months to get on the dance floor of the milonga of Almagro, I didn't dare to, and I went every Sunday only to watch. One breathed an air of respect that cannot be found now."

**On lost density:**
> "Before, people worked with precision and a particular aesthetic, in a functional and mechanical way that gave it a form, and a style. Making a movement or taking a step implied an expression of the entire body. Currently, not only has the essence been lost but the weight of the dance as well, its density and importance."

### Interview #4: Gazzetta Tango

On what exhausts him about tango:
> "So many things… traditional tango, tango nuevo… milongas in some countries where they serve only water and fruit juices… hysterical women, showoff men… the business… the lack of creativity, the absence of new dancers with personality… the bent hand and the other one on women's asses… selective marathons, the 'famous' DJs! The enormous number of festivals everywhere… the lack of eagerness to learn… should I go on?"

On the future:
> "Tango today is installed… there's no going back, it's a machine that can't be stopped… The future today lies in education, understanding, respect and the love we owe this dance… Tango is a world full of its own life, difficult… the night, the drugs, the alcohol, the promiscuity… It's not just about doing a good colgada… it's about understanding the heart and where the soul of what we're dancing is."

**His preferences:**
- **Top three tangos:** Como dos extraños, Quedémonos aquí, Pasional
- **Top three orchestras:** [Pugliese](/people/osvaldo-pugliese), [Di Sarli](/people/carlos-di-sarli), and "el gran Astor" ([Piazzolla](/people/astor-piazzolla))
- **Top three male dancers:** [Gustavo Naveira](/people/gustavo-naveira), Julio Balmaceda, Osvaldo Soto
- **Top three female dancers:** [Milena Plebs](/people/milena-plebs), Guillermina Quiroga, Vanina Bilous

### Statement #5: Facebook Manifesto, August 2015

He mourns the lost milongas of Almagro, Parakultural Centre in Chacabuco, Arlequines, and La Viruta "in its Golden Age":

> "A time when aesthetics weren't so important… a time when we were learning to dance, and we told ourselves that true authenticity lay in discovering our own dance."

On the proliferation of teachers:
> "There are countless 'maestros' and a ridiculous number of 'professionals', which is a double-edged sword."

On competitions and style:
> "It's difficult to understand why we have this 'dance factory' for competitions. What happened to tango…? To dancing? Having fun… seduction… playfulness… creativity…? Why teach styles? Because this limits our bodies, arms, backs, feet and necks."

The closing:
> "Don't be cardboard cut-outs, guys. It's very cold and empty. You won't find tango by chasing a prize, or money, or fame. The Tango is within us… as it's always been."

## Evolution of Views

The chronological progression of Chicho's public statements traces a remarkable journey:

- **2005**: Energetic and revolutionary, already noting that Tango Nuevo was "a question of marketing" but still primarily an innovator
- **2008**: Articulated the 50/50 partnership philosophy in detail
- **2009**: The turning point — a public confession of responsibility for tango's loss of essence, declaring himself "a milonguero again"
- **2015**: Full-throated critique of competition culture, style conformity, and the "dance factory"
- **2020s**: Maintains this traditionalist-revolutionary position while continuing to tour and teach globally
