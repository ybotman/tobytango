# Mariano "Chicho" Frúmboli

Argentine tango dancer and innovator, widely regarded as one of the founders of tango nuevo and known worldwide as "the master of the rebound." A former rock drummer who came to tango at 23, Chicho brought a musician's ear and a rebel's instinct to the dance, pioneering the 50/50 partnership dynamic and expanding tango's movement vocabulary from Buenos Aires to the stages of Europe and beyond. With his long-time partner Juana Sepúlveda, he continues to perform and teach internationally.

---

## Biography

### Early Life and Musical Roots

Mariano Frúmboli was born on September 21, 1970, in Buenos Aires, Argentina. His father was a fine arts professor who studied guitar and listened to Astor Piazzolla — giving young Chicho his first exposure to tango through music rather than dance. But Chicho's own path led to rock, not tango. He began studying music at age 13, attending the David Lebón Music School in Buenos Aires, and spent 14 years as a rock drummer. He also studied theater with the acclaimed actress and teacher Cristina Banegas from 1992 to 1998.

### Conversion to Tango (1993)

In 1993, Chicho attended a tango class taught by Ricardo Barrios and Victoria Vieyra. The experience was transformative:

> "I was into rock-and-roll; I had long hair and played the drums. I hated tango, I didn't like it one bit. But when I went to take a class with Ricardo Barrios and Victoria Vieyra, I embraced my dance partner for the first time and I got goose bumps."

Victoria Vieyra introduced him to the Tango Investigation Group led by Gustavo Naveira and Fabián Salas. Naveira later said of Chicho: "With only six months of study, he was amazing as a tango dancer" and "Chicho was absolutely the best dancer I ever saw."

While Naveira and Salas traveled abroad to teach, Chicho stayed behind, absorbing and practicing, waiting for them to return with new information to push the investigation further.

### Europe and Expansion (1998–present)

In October 1998, Chicho arrived in Europe with Victoria Vieyra. He settled first in France, then Belgium, where he taught tango nuevo. In Paris, he taught with Camilla Saracens to theater artists for three years, and spent much of the 2000s expanding tango's movement vocabulary from his European base.

## Style & Philosophy

### The Master of the Rebound

Chicho is known as "the master of the rebound" (*rebote*). His signature technique draws on the swing rhythms embedded in his rock-and-roll background. As one description puts it: "He'll send you out and pull you back in, or send you out and come around to your other side."

### The 50/50 Partnership

Chicho pioneered a partnership dynamic where the woman is not simply following but co-creating the dance in real time:

> "I don't think that woman is going to occupy more or less space, if not that the couple takes on more strength and power when it is a couple, with an equality between the two, and today that is really a division of 50/50."
> — Brisbane House of Tango interview, November 2021

> "Today women have much more participation than before, when men gave orders with the hand in the back of the woman or when dancing was all choreographed. Now women have much greater freedom within what happens inside of the embrace. The relation between men and women is now much more a feedback one."

### Musicality and Improvisation

Chicho's 14 years as a drummer are central to his dancing. He prioritizes musicality and improvisation over choreography:

> "In addition to creating you are leading your partner... I know that phrase and how long it lasts, I know when it has to end and I prepare the movement along the way so that it fits perfectly with the music."

> "Improvisation gives things that cannot be had when choreography is prepared."

### Tango as Universal

Chicho has consistently argued that tango must be a world dance, not a Buenos Aires possession:

> "My idea, and that of Gustavo Naveira and Fabián Salas as well, was always that the tango needed to be popular in the world. Obviously, it is born from one country and has its origins, but it needs to be universal. It cannot belong only to Buenos Aires."

### Adaptability

Chicho criticizes dancers who cannot adapt to new partners, calling it "a great error" and arguing that if dancers could adapt, tango would evolve much faster. Different partners propose different ways of dancing, and his body must adapt.

## Notable Quotes

### On Discovering Tango
> "I was into rock-and-roll; I had long hair and played the drums. I hated tango, I didn't like it one bit. But when I went to take a class with Ricardo Barrios and Victoria Vieyra, I embraced my dance partner for the first time and I got goose bumps."

### On Tango Nuevo
> "Tango Nuevo does exist, but it has for a very long time. Copes was dancing a new tango, Miguel Angel Zotto had a new tango. Every once in a while there is someone who appears and proposes something new."

> "To think that 'Tango Nuevo' is something that occurred only 10 years ago is a commercial exploitation that we owe to the festival organizers."
> — March 2008

### The Famous Self-Critique (El Tangauta, December 2009)
> "The problem is that we missed something in the teaching, I take total responsibility. I can't pass on what I have learned. I was crazy about creating, because I saw a new vein in the evolution of the movement. I threw myself into that, and I lost the way to be able to pass on the tango essence."
> — Interview with Milena Plebs, El Tangauta

> "Not only has the essence been lost but the weight of the dance as well, its density and importance."

> "The traditionalists complain about the modern ones contending that they don't dance tango, instead they do gymnastics, and the modern dancers complain that the others got stuck in time. There is no fusion, it is one group against the other, and it makes me sad because in reality we are all together."

### On Connection and Communication
> "If he only thinks about his own figure, step, and elegance while forgetting his partner, there will be disconnection, so communication must be subtle and corporal — not just through the hands."

> "If she cannot feel comfortable dancing, then I cannot dance."

### On Authenticity
> "Don't be cardboard cut-outs, guys. It's very cold and empty."

### On the Future of Tango (El Tangauta, 2009)
> "We are living the beginning of a powerful era... the genre is here to stay, there is no way that it will become hidden or marginalized again."
