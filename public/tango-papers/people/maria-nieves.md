# María Nieves Rego

One half of tango's most legendary partnership, María Nieves rose from poverty in Saavedra to become the defining female tango dancer of the 20th century. Her 50-year collaboration with Juan Carlos Copes — through marriage, divorce, and decades of creative tension — produced "Tango Argentino," the show that saved tango from extinction. With her own inimitable style and fierce independence, she proved that the follower in tango gives equal value to the dance.

> "The first time I danced the tango, it entered my skin through my feet, passed from my skin to my blood and through my blood to my heart."

---

## Biography

### Childhood in Poverty

María Nieves Rego was born on September 6, 1934, in the Saavedra district of Buenos Aires, the daughter of modest Galician emigrants. She grew up in poverty, working as a maid in La Boca from childhood.

### Discovery at Fifteen

At age 15, María was discovered by Juan Carlos Copes at the Estrella dance club in Maldonado. He was 17; she was 14. Their partnership would span the next five decades.

### Early Competitions and Debut

María won a dance contest at Luna Park, judged by orchestra leader Juan D'Arienzo and racing legend Juan Manuel Fangio. She débuted at the Nacional theater in the Conjunto Juvenil alongside impresario Carlos Petit, and later performed at the luxurious Tabaris cabaret.

### Marriage and Beyond

Nieves and Copes married in Las Vegas. The marriage did not last — they divorced — yet they continued as dance partners for decades. This tumultuous relationship became the subject of the 2015 documentary "Un Tango Más."

> "If I have to say something about him, it is that he was a son of a bitch with me, but there will never be a male dancer like Juan Carlos Copes."

### International Breakthrough (1959)

In October 1959, the pair flew to Puerto Rico where they packed out Club Flamboyan in San Juan with "Compañía Argentina Tangolandia," with Astor Piazzolla serving as musical director. The tour continued to New York, Chicago, and Washington.

They appeared on the Ed Sullivan Show and performed in Las Vegas, bringing authentic tango to American television audiences.

### Tango Argentino (1983–1986)

In the 1980s, working with directors Claudio Segovia and Héctor Orezzoli, Nieves and Copes created and starred in the musical "Tango Argentino." The show premièred at the Festival d'Automne in Paris in 1983, then moved to Broadway in 1985 at the Mark Hellinger Theatre.

The run had to be extended — they were only booked for a week. The show moved to another theatre and continued until the following year, running 199 performances and igniting a worldwide tango revival.

### White House Performance

María Nieves was invited to perform at Ronald Reagan's White House birthday celebration, representing Argentine tango at the highest levels of American society.

### Later Career

After "Tango Argentino," Nieves appeared in "Tanguera" and returned to the Nacional theater stage — 50 years after her initial performances there. She remains active as a tango ambassador and living legend.

### Documentary: Un Tango Más (2015)

The documentary "Un Tango Más" (Our Last Tango), directed by German Kral, depicted the complex 50-year relationship between Nieves and Copes. The director described María as:

> "A woman with a lot of character and a unique sense of humor. She is spontaneous, real, intelligent, seductive, respectful, beautiful, sincere and very difficult at the same time."

In the film, María reflected:

> "If I were reborn, I would be a tango dancer. I would do everything except be with Juan."

## Style & Philosophy

### "The Greatest Rhythmical Cell"

Nieves and Copes together were described as "the greatest rhythmical cell of dancing tango" — a partnership where technical precision met emotional intensity.

### Equal Value

María embodied Copes' philosophy that "the man and the woman each give fifty percent." She was never merely a follower, but a creative partner who added her own value to every dance.

### On Tango's Power

> "The first time I danced the tango, it entered my skin through my feet, passed from my skin to my blood and through my blood to my heart."

### On Artistic Transformation

Remarkably, Nieves attributes her improvement as an artist to the hatred between the two dancers. The tension fueled rather than destroyed their creative partnership.
