# Astor Piazzolla

The tango assassin. Or the tango savior — depending on whom you ask. Astor Piazzolla revolutionized traditional tango into nuevo tango, incorporating jazz and classical music into the form. He received death threats, was shot at by tango extremists, and was accused of "murdering" tango. His response: "I performed plastic surgery on it." With 750 compositions, from *Libertango* to *Five Tango Sensations*, he transformed tango from dance music into concert art. Today, he is Argentina's most famous composer.

---

## Biography

### Mar del Plata to New York (1921–1936)

Astor Pantaleón Piazzolla was born on March 11, 1921, in Mar del Plata, Argentina. In 1925, his family moved to Greenwich Village in New York City, where he would spend his formative years.

### The Pawnshop Bandoneón (1929)

Piazzolla received his first bandoneón at age eight, when his father spotted one in a New York pawnshop. The following year, he began studying with Hungarian classical pianist Béla Wilda — a student of Rachmaninoff — who taught him to play Bach on the bandoneón. This classical foundation would later distinguish his approach to tango.

### Return to Argentina (1936)

At fifteen, Piazzolla returned to Mar del Plata. At seventeen, he relocated to Buenos Aires to pursue music professionally.

### With Aníbal Troilo (1939–1944)

Piazzolla joined the orchestra of Aníbal Troilo, the supreme bandoneón of Buenos Aires. But Troilo began to fear that Piazzolla's advanced musical ideas might undermine the orchestra's style. Tensions mounted until 1944, when Piazzolla announced his intention to leave. Despite the break, Troilo would later express both criticism and admiration: "He would often criticize Piazzolla's work, but at the same time express great affection and admiration for him."

### First Orchestra (1946)

In 1946, Piazzolla formed his own orchestra and began experimenting with tango structure.

### Classical Studies (1949–1952)

In 1949, he disbanded his orchestra to pursue classical composition. In 1951, he won a composing contest with his symphonic piece *Buenos Aires*, which earned him a scholarship to study in Paris under the legendary Nadia Boulanger.

### Boulanger's Advice (1951–1952)

Boulanger encouraged him to "remain true to himself and to continue his experiments with the tango" — not to abandon his roots for classical music. This advice proved decisive: Piazzolla returned to Argentina in 1955 and began developing nuevo tango.

### Breaking the Orquesta Típica (1955)

In 1955, Piazzolla formed his Orquesta de Cuerdas (String Orchestra) and his revolutionary Octeto Buenos Aires — with two bandonéones, two violins, double bass, cello, piano, and electric guitar. This ensemble "effectively broke the mould of the traditional orquesta típica and created a new sound akin to chamber music."

### Quinteto Nuevo Tango (1960)

After time in the United States (1958–1960), Piazzolla formed the influential **Quinteto Nuevo Tango** featuring violin, electric guitar, piano, double bass, and bandoneón. This quintet became the vehicle for his most famous compositions.

### Death Threats and Controversy

The shift to nuevo tango resulted in death threats. He was even shot at by tango extremists. "If you want to change the tango," he said, "you had better learn boxing, or some other martial art."

### Paris Years (1974–1985)

Piazzolla moved to Paris in 1974, where his work gained wider European acceptance. *Libertango* (1974) exemplified his new direction — the title merging "liberty" and "tango" to mark his transition.

### Final Return and Late Works (1985–1992)

He returned permanently to Argentina in 1985. His late masterworks include the bandoneón concerto (1979) and *Five Tango Sensations* for bandoneón and string quartet (1989), commissioned by the Kronos Quartet.

### Death (1992)

Piazzolla died on July 4, 1992, in Buenos Aires, at age 71.

## Philosophy

### "For the Ear, Not the Feet"

> "For me, tango was always for the ear rather than the feet."

This encapsulates Piazzolla's transformation of tango from dance music to concert art.

### "I Performed Plastic Surgery"

> "I still can't believe that some pseudocritics continue to accuse me of having murdered tango. They have it backward. They should look at me as the saviour of tango. I performed plastic surgery on it."

### On Facing Resistance

> "If you want to change the tango, you had better learn boxing, or some other martial art."

## Musical Innovation

### Breaking the Orquesta Típica

The Octeto Buenos Aires (1955) broke the mold: two bandonéones, two violins, double bass, cello, piano, and electric guitar. This created "a new sound akin to chamber music."

### Nuevo Tango Elements

- Jazz harmonies and improvisation
- Classical counterpoint
- New rhythmic structures
- Electric guitar in ensemble
- Chamber music sensibility

---

## Legacy

### Initial Rejection, Later Acceptance

Piazzolla's nuevo tango made him controversial in Argentina "both musically and politically." But his music gained acceptance in Europe and North America, and eventually liberal segments of Argentine society embraced his reworking of the form.

### Film and Media

His music has been featured in countless film scores, television programs, and commercials since the 1970s–80s.

### Influence

Piazzolla influenced a new generation of tango composers. Today, he is Argentina's most internationally recognized classical-adjacent composer.
