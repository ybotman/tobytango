# Juan Carlos Copes

The man who brought Argentine tango to Broadway and ignited a worldwide renaissance. Juan Carlos Copes, with his partner María Nieves, transformed tango from a neighborhood social dance into a theatrical art form that captivated international audiences. His choreography for "Tango Argentino" (1985) earned a Tony nomination and introduced authentic tango to millions who had only seen Hollywood's distortions. The "Copes method" and "estilo Copes-Nieves" influenced generations of dancers.

---

## Biography

### Early Life in Mataderos

Juan Carlos Copes was born on May 31, 1931, in the Mataderos neighborhood of Buenos Aires. His father was a tango music enthusiast, while his mother encouraged artistic pursuits. He received formal dance training from local instructors and practiced extensively in neighborhood milongas.

### Meeting María Nieves (early 1950s)

Copes met María Nieves Rego when he was 17 and she was 14. Their legendary partnership would span over 50 years, revolutionizing tango through innovative choreography and passionate performances. They eventually married in Las Vegas, though the marriage did not last — yet they continued as dance partners for decades.

### Rise in Buenos Aires (1950s–1960s)

Through the 1950s and 1960s, Copes and Nieves performed at local competitions, Teatro Astral, and the prestigious Teatro Colón in Buenos Aires. Copes began developing what would become known as the "Copes method" — a systematic approach to tango choreography and teaching.

### Tango Argentino and Broadway (1983–1985)

Working with directors Claudio Segovia and Héctor Orezzoli, Copes helped assemble fellow tangueros into a musical revue that premiered at the Festival d'Automne in Paris in 1983. The show, "Tango Argentino," opened on Broadway at the Mark Hellinger Theatre on October 9, 1985, running for 199 performances until March 30, 1986.

Copes served as both lead dancer and choreographer. The production:
- Earned Tony nominations including Best Musical and Best Choreography
- Won Copes recognition as "dancer of the year" in the New York Times
- Sparked a worldwide tango revival that continues today

> "The tango is the man and woman in search of each other. It is the search for an embrace, a way to be together."

### A New Philosophy

Copes introduced a revolutionary approach to tango partnership:

> "We presented the man and the woman each fifty percent. In order for the woman to not just follow, but also give her own value to the dance."

This philosophy of equality distinguished his choreography from earlier theatrical tango.

### Film Appearances

- **Tango Bar** (1987)
- **The Tango Lesson** (1997) — directed by Sally Potter

### Teaching Legacy

Copes created the "estilo Copes-Nieves" (Copes-Nieves style) and taught many of the next generation's leading dancers:

- **Robert Duvall** — Academy Award-winning actor
- **Julio Bocca** — ballet star
- **Mikhail Baryshnikov** — legendary dancer
- **Liza Minnelli** — entertainer
- **Miguel Ángel Zotto** — founded Tango x 2
- **Mora Godoy** — contemporary tango star
- **Johana Copes** — his daughter, continued his legacy

### Later Years

In his final years, Copes continued performing with his daughter Johana. The 2015 documentary "Un Tango Más" (Our Last Tango), directed by German Kral, depicted his complex 50-year relationship with María Nieves.

### Death

Juan Carlos Copes died on January 16, 2021, at age 89, from COVID-19 complications in Buenos Aires.

## Style & Philosophy

### The Copes Method

Copes developed a systematic approach to choreography that transformed tango from improvised social dance to structured theatrical art. His method enabled complex choreography while preserving tango's essential emotional content.

### Fifty-Fifty Partnership

> "We presented the man and the woman each fifty percent."

Unlike earlier theatrical tango that emphasized male dominance, Copes choreographed for equality — the woman not just following but contributing her own value to the dance.

### On Tango's Essence

> "The tango is the man and woman in search of each other. It is the search for an embrace, a way to be together, when the man feels that he is a male and the woman feels that she is female, without machismo."

### Theatrical Innovation

Copes was the first to create fully choreographed tango stage shows, incorporating theatrical elements like lighting, costumes, and narrative structure while maintaining authentic tango vocabulary.
