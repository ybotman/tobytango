# Héctor Pellozo (Cachirulo)

The rule enforcer. Héctor Pellozo organizes Cachirulo — Buenos Aires' most famously traditional milonga. Born in 1937, he is one of the most famous living milongueros, featured in the documentary *Milongueros*. He does what no one else does: announces the dance floor rules and enforces them, using red and yellow cards when dancers break the codes. His laminated rule sheets, translated into 10 languages, are the "El Cachi trademark." With partner Norma Zugasti, he preserves milonga tradition in an era of change.

---

## Biography

### Born in the Golden Age (1937)

Héctor Pellozo was born July 5, 1937 — during the Golden Age of tango. He grew up with tango, becoming one of the milongueros who would later preserve its traditions.

### Partnership with Norma Zugasti

Héctor organizes Cachirulo with his partner Norma Zugasti. Together they have created one of Buenos Aires' most tradition-conscious milongas.

### The Cachirulo Milonga

The milonga takes its name from a famous Troilo tango. Over the years, it has moved through several locations:
- Centro Región Leonesa
- Plaza Bohemia (Maipu 444)
- Club Villa Malcolm (Villa Crespo)
- Obelisco Tango

### The Rule Enforcer

Héctor wants dancers to enjoy themselves, so "he does what no one has done before — he announces the dance floor rules and sees that they are enforced."

His methods:
- **Laminated Rule Sheets** — "The El Cachi trademark" — listing the golden rules of the milonga, translated into 10 languages
- **Red and Yellow Cards** — Used "always discreetly and amusingly" when dancers break the rules

### Seating Tradition

The fact that Héctor seats couples who attend together in the last row is not arbitrary, but "in accord with the unwritten rules of the traditional milonga."

### Documentary Recognition

Héctor is one of the most famous living milongueros, featured in interviews and the documentary *Milongueros*.

## The Codes of Cachirulo

### The Golden Rules

The laminated rule sheets (in 10 languages) enforce traditional milonga codes:
- Line of dance
- Cabeceo for invitation
- Tanda structure
- Floor craft
- Appropriate behavior

### Enforcement

Red and yellow cards are used — discreetly and with humor — for violations.

---

## Philosophy

### Tradition as Joy

Héctor enforces the codes not as authoritarianism but to ensure everyone enjoys the milonga. The rules exist to create a better dance experience for all.

### Preservation Through Action

In an era when many milongas have abandoned traditional codes, Cachirulo maintains them — teaching a new generation how milongas once functioned.
