# Mingo Pugliese

The teacher's teacher. Mingo Pugliese was one of the most respected tangueros of the Argentine tango revival, known for devising the 8-count molinete teaching method that became standard worldwide. Born in Villa Devoto in 1932, he started dancing in the salons in 1948 and never stopped — even through tango's darkest decades. A disciple of Petroleo, Mingo bridged the old and new styles, influencing generations of instructors including Alberto Paz and Valorie Hart.

---

## Biography

### Early Life in Villa Devoto

Born in 1932 in Villa Devoto, northwest of Buenos Aires, Mingo was the youngest of three sons of Gregorio and Maria Pugliese. His father was a consummate vals criollo dancer — a dance similar to the Viennese waltz but danced much closer with knees tied together to move in very small spaces.

### Starting in the Salons (1948)

As a teenager in 1948, Mingo began dancing tango in the salons, mixing with prominent dancers of that era. This was still the Golden Age, and he absorbed the authentic milonga culture directly.

### Disciple of Petroleo

Mingo considers himself a disciple of Carlos Estevez, known as "Petroleo" — one of the great innovators who transformed tango instruction in the 1940s. He was part of the renovators' movement that modernized teaching methodology while preserving tradition.

### Partnership with Esther

Mingo and his wife Esther Pugliese became a renowned teaching couple, developing a method that stressed good posture, careful foot placement, and extensive body communication.

### The Revival Years

After the Argentine dictatorship fell in 1983, Mingo was at the forefront of the tango revival. He taught and danced a style that incorporated both old and new elements, making him an essential bridge figure.

---

## The 8-Count Molinete

Mingo is most noted for devising a teaching method for the 8-point giro (turn). This structured approach to teaching the molinete with sacadas in open embrace emerged from innovations by a group of dancers led by Petroleo.

The method was adopted by his students Alberto Paz and Valorie Hart, who helped spread it internationally. Today, the 8-count molinete is a foundational element in tango instruction worldwide.

## Teaching Philosophy

### Posture and Placement

Mingo emphasized:
- Good posture
- Careful foot placement
- Extensive body communication
- Development of improvisation skills

### The Giro as Foundation

Eight-count left and right giros (turns) were central to Mingo and Esther's teaching style. This systematic approach made complex movements accessible to students.

### Bridging Eras

Mingo taught a style that incorporated both old and new approaches, respecting tradition while embracing evolution.

---

## On Preserving Tango

Mingo defended those who kept tango alive during its decline:

> "I respect very much and I don't allow anybody to talk bad about [Juan Carlos Copes, Eduardo Arquimbau, and Virulazo] for their dedication when nobody cared about the Tango anymore."
