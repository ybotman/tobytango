# Daniel Trenner

The Johnny Appleseed of tango. Daniel Trenner discovered tango in Buenos Aires in 1987 while teaching jazz and improvisation at the San Martín Theatre's State School of Contemporary Dance — and became one of tango's most important documentarians. An "accidental anthropologist," he filmed and interviewed the last generation of Golden Age milongueros in the 1980s and 90s. He toured more than 100 cities on 4 continents over 20 years, produced 80+ instructional video volumes with 40+ artists, and led over 1,500 dancers to Buenos Aires through his Bridge to the Tango tours. His archive preserves tango history that would otherwise be lost.

---

## Biography

### Discovery in Buenos Aires (1987)

Trenner "happened on Tango in Buenos Aires in 1987" while teaching jazz and improvisation at the State School of Contemporary Dance at the Teatro San Martín. This chance encounter began a lifelong dedication to tango.

### Education

He holds a Master of Education in Dance (1984), bringing formal pedagogy training to his tango teaching — unusual in the first generation of American tango teachers.

### Studying with Everyone

During his 12+ years of regular Buenos Aires visits (1997–2009), Trenner studied "with just about every teacher I could find." Rather than specializing in one style, he absorbed the full breadth of tango's diversity.

### Filming the Elders

As an "accidental anthropologist," Trenner filmed and interviewed many of the last generation of Golden Age survivors in the 1980s and 90s. He documented milongas, workshops, private lessons, and social gatherings during tango's revival — creating an irreplaceable archive.

### The Johnny Appleseed of Tango

From the late 1980s through 2000s, Trenner toured as a tango teacher for 20 years, visiting more than 100 cities on 4 continents. He was among the first generation of revival teachers spreading tango globally.

### Bridge to the Tango Tours (1993–2000)

Trenner created the first tango tours to Buenos Aires with his **Bridge to the Tango** program, bringing more than 1,500 people to dance in 7 countries. The final two years (1999–2000) included expanded group tours and advanced trainings.

### Philosophy: Experience the Genetics

> "The idea of Bridge to the Tango was to provide, through direct visits, an opportunity for the visiting dancer to experience the breadth and variety of the tango's genetics rather than an intensive focus on any one style."

### Instructional Videos

He produced more than **80 volumes of tango instructional video** featuring more than **40 artists** — documenting the teaching of the revival era's most important figures.

### The Tango Archive

The Archive was originally created as part of International Tango Productions (1996–2002), the company that ran the Tango Catalogue, Bridge to the Tango Tours, and his touring and local classes.

Since 2010, Trenner has been digitizing and cataloging thousands of hours of VHS and Hi8 footage — preserving tango history from "when Tango almost disappeared with the Last Generation of the Golden Age."

## The Archive

### Contents

- Interviews with milongueros
- Milonga footage from the revival era
- Workshops and private lessons
- Social gatherings
- Thousands of hours of VHS and Hi8 footage

### Mission

"Knowing, studying and sharing the history, origins and evolution of Argentine Tango" — the Archive documents tango's revival through the 70s, 80s, and 90s.

### Bridge to the Tango Video (BttTV)

80+ volumes of instructional video featuring 40+ artists, preserving teaching methodologies from the revival era.

---

## Philosophy

### Experiential Learning

> "Learning tango is best done experientially — by visiting with dancers in their natural environment."

### Diversity Over Specialization

Rather than intensive focus on one style, Trenner emphasized experiencing "the breadth and variety of the tango's genetics."

### Personalized Training Tradition

> "Personalized training is a tradition in Tango Dancing, going all the way back to the Golden Years in Buenos Aires, and continuing during the last 30 years of Tango's Modern Revival."

---

## Significance

### First Generation Revival Teacher

Trenner was "one of the important characters of the first generation of revival youth" who brought tango to the wider world.

### Documentarian

His films preserve the teaching and movement of dancers who are no longer alive — an irreplaceable historical resource.

### Bridge Builder

By bringing 1,500+ people to Buenos Aires, he created direct connections between foreign dancers and Argentine masters.
