# Pedro "Tete" Rusconi

Argentine milonguero and guardian of tango salón, famous for his extraordinary vals and his role in bringing close-embrace tango to the United States. Born in the barrio of Pompeya and dancing since the 1950s, Tete became one of the most influential teachers of traditional milonguero style, working alongside Susana Miller to give the style its name. His collaboration with choreographer Pina Bausch brought tango to the world of contemporary dance, and his partnership with Silvia Ceriani trained dancers across five continents. He danced his last tango at El Beso, two days before his 74th birthday.

## Biography

### Early Life and Barrio Roots

Pedro Alberto Rusconi was born on January 9, 1936, in the barrio of Pompeya, Buenos Aires. He began dancing at age 14, learning salon tango not through formal instruction but through careful observation of the milongueros around him. This immersive, apprentice-style learning would later inform his own teaching philosophy.

He lived the last years of his life in a boarding house at Pringles 84 in the barrio of Almagro.

### Dancing Since the Fifties

Tete had been dancing since the 1950s — through the Golden Age's twilight, the dark years of tango's decline, and into its revival. He was a living repository of the social dance traditions that younger generations were struggling to reconstruct.

### Vals Specialty

Tete developed a signature style for waltzes with distinctive footwork and exceptional musicality. His vals was characterized by an "incredibly effortless, floating quality" — the ability to swirl right and left, spin on a dime, and maintain fluidity while improvising. He and his partners were particularly noted for their beautiful vals.

### The Name "Milonguero Style" (1994–1995)

In the mid-1990s, Tete was instrumental in bringing close-embrace tango to North America. Working with Susana Miller, who coined the term "milonguero style," and alongside Oscar "Cacho" Dante, Tete helped develop a new didactic system to teach traditional close-embrace tango.

In 1994, Daniel Trenner taught at Stanford University's Tango Week, describing the Rusconi/Miller milonguero style as "the only authentic Buenos Aires tango." This endorsement helped establish milonguero style in the San Francisco Bay Area and beyond.

Tete was one of the first milongueros to teach close embrace in the United States, under the auspices of Susana Miller.

### Partnership with Silvia Ceriani (1995–2010)

In 1995, Tete began his partnership with Silvia Ceriani, a writer and artist. They traveled together to Europe in 1996, and within a few years were teaching around the world — with a special focus on musicality and dance sense.

Together they produced instructional videos covering turns, enrosques, sacadas, and the concept of dancing to tango waltz music.

### Pina Bausch and International Recognition (1997)

In 1997, the legendary German choreographer Pina Bausch invited Tete to Europe. He danced in her piece "Nur Du" (Only You) for three years running. She also invited him for the 25th anniversary celebration of Tanztheater Wuppertal.

This collaboration brought Tete's embodied knowledge of traditional tango into dialogue with contemporary dance, exposing European audiences to authentic milonguero movement.

### Connection to Tango Nuevo

Though Tete represented the traditional milonguero world, he was connected to the nuevo generation. Chicho Frúmboli studied with Tete during his early development, before Victoria Vieyra introduced him to Naveira and Salas's investigation group.

### El Beso and Final Days

Tete was among the legendary milongueros who made El Beso a meeting point, alongside Flaco Dany, El Nene, Tito Rocca, Tito Franquelo, and El Pibe Sarandí.

On January 6, 2010, Tete danced his last dance at El Beso milonga in Buenos Aires. He died in the early morning of January 7, 2010 — two days short of his 74th birthday. His life completed its full cycle.

## Style & Philosophy

### Tango Salón in Close Embrace

Tete led a revival of Tango Salón in close embrace from the Golden Age. His style featured:
- Natural walking technique
- Elegant swaying movements
- Shorter steps with quicker, more complicated footwork
- More and faster turns
- The distinctive "opening of the arms" using chest to lead

> "There is nothing more beautiful than the Tango Salón."
> — Tete Rusconi, 2002

### Musicality First

Tete's teaching philosophy prioritized music over steps. In a letter distributed at milongas, he wrote:

> "I believe that many are disguising tango as something that it isn't. Tango is music, and it doesn't begin with steps. We shouldn't commit the mistake of not teaching how to walk different musical rhythms to recognize each orchestra."

> "Tango has a thousand ways to be danced but first let's step on the ground because there you'll find the energy."

### On Teaching

> "Many who are teaching have to learn how to dance tango first, then give everything in order not to cheat their students or damage their reputation as teachers. Tango isn't a business, although many see it as such."

### On Learning

Tete believed that dancers choose their teachers, and that tango becomes "a part of you that you cannot detach." After learning the fundamentals, each person develops their own style.

> "The followers learn the tango by dancing it."

### Advice to the Young

> "Put in your dancing all of the beauty that tango has."
