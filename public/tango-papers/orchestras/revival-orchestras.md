# Revival Orchestras: New Generations (1990–Present)

**After 35 years of near-silence, young Argentine musicians began rebuilding the orquesta típica tradition in the 1990s.** These revival orchestras face a paradox: they compete with 50,000 perfect Golden Age recordings while struggling to find bandoneóns and trained musicians.

---

## The First Wave (1990–2000)

### Color Tango (1990)
Founded by **Roberto Álvarez**, Pugliese's first bandoneón, Color Tango preserves the maestro's arrangements note-for-note. This faithful reproduction approach established one model for revival: meticulous recreation of classic orchestrations.

### El Arranque (1996)
One of the earliest revival orchestras, El Arranque took a traditional approach focused on the dance floor. Their recordings became milonga staples alongside Golden Age tracks.

---

## The Punk Generation (2000s)

### Orquesta Típica Fernández Fierro (2001)
The most radical departure from revival orthodoxy. OTFF brought punk energy and a cooperative ownership model to tango, operating their own venue (Club Atlético Fernández Fierro) and reaching audiences who would never enter a traditional milonga. Their aggressive style divides traditionalists but undeniably expanded tango's reach.

### Sexteto Milonguero (2006–2020)
The opposite approach: laser-focused on dance-floor practicality, faithful to Golden Age sound. Led by bandoneónist Hernán Castiello, they became a favorite at milongas worldwide before disbanding in 2020.

---

## International Expansion (2010s)

### Bandonegro (Poland, 2010)
Proof that non-Argentines can play authentic tango at the highest level. The Polish orchestra tours the international festival circuit, demonstrating that the tradition has truly globalized.

### Orquesta Rascacielos, Tango Bardo
Contemporary revival orchestras continuing to push the form forward while respecting its roots.

---

## The Electronic Branch

### Gotan Project (2001) & Bajofondo (2002)
These electronic tango collectives created music for listeners, not dancers. While controversial among traditionalists, they introduced millions to tango sounds who might never have encountered it otherwise.

---

## The Challenge

Revival orchestras face structural obstacles:
- **The recordings are perfect**: Why hire a live orchestra when you can play Di Sarli's 1940s recordings?
- **Bandoneón scarcity**: The Alfred Arnold factory closed in 1948; authentic instruments are rare and expensive
- **Training pipeline**: Rebuilt through institutions like Escuela de Música Popular de Avellaneda and Orquesta Escuela Emilio Balcarce

Despite these challenges, the revival has created a living tango music scene that didn't exist in 1985.
