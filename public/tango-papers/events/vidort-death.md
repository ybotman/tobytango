# Vidort Dies (May 21, 2006)

> **HITM FLAG:** Primary sources for Vidort are mainly tango community blogs, particularly tangoandchaos.org by Rick McGarrey. Some details may reflect oral history rather than documented fact.

Ricardo Vidort died on a Sunday in Santa Fe, New Mexico — a long way from the Buenos Aires barrios where he learned to dance. He was 76, and they called him "The Last Compadrito."

## The Compadrito

The compadrito was the original tango archetype — the streetwise, knife-carrying dandy of the old neighborhoods, the man who danced tango not as art or exercise but as a way of being in the world. Vidort embodied it.

Born August 30, 1929, he grew up in the barrio culture of Buenos Aires, learned to dance as a youth, and carried the attitudes, the stories, the swagger of the old tango world into the modern era. He could tell you about knife fights at early milongas and practicing steps on street corners — and some of it might even have been true. As Rick McGarrey wrote: **"He lived like one of the old compadritos, and he saw the world through their eyes."**

## The Dancing

What was indisputably real was his dancing. Vidort didn't dance to the rhythm of the music — he danced the *phrase*. He incorporated compás into giros and corridas in ways no one else did, adding pauses, curving his runs, staying on one foot while marking beats with the other.

He was one of only two milongueros (along with Gavito) who learned English, which allowed him to teach internationally with an intimacy most Argentine masters couldn't achieve.

## End

In his final years, he was cared for by his companion Ewa in Santa Fe. His last words to her: **"God threw a stone at me that changed into a diamond. I must have done something good in life to have met you."**

When Vidort walked into a milonga, everyone — admirers and critics alike — perked up. You knew you were in the right place. You knew you were in a real milonga.
