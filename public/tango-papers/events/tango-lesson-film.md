# 'The Tango Lesson' Film (1997)

In 1997, British filmmaker Sally Potter released a movie that did something no Hollywood production had managed: it showed the world what Argentine tango actually looked like.

## The Story

*The Tango Lesson* was semi-autobiographical — Potter played a version of herself, a director struggling with writer's block who wanders into a Paris theater and sees Argentine dancer Pablo Verón performing. She is transfixed. They strike a deal: he'll make her a tango dancer, she'll make him a movie star.

What follows is a meditation on leading and following, control and surrender, filmed mostly in gorgeous black and white by cinematographer Robby Müller, with the "real life" tango scenes contrasting against vivid color sequences from the fictional screenplay Potter is trying to write.

## Recognition

The film premiered at the Venice Film Festival, won Best Film at the Mar del Plata Film Festival, and was distributed by Sony Pictures Classics. Roger Ebert praised it warmly: **"Most dances are for people who are falling in love. The tango is a dance for those who have survived it."**

## The Dancers

Crucially, the cast included not just Verón — whom *Libération* called "the best dancer in the world" and *The New York Times* said had "the feet of God" — but also Gustavo Naveira and Fabián Salas, genuine masters of Buenos Aires tango.

## The Soundtrack

The soundtrack featured Gardel, Piazzolla (with Yo-Yo Ma on cello), D'Arienzo, and Pugliese.

## Impact

For audiences who had only ever seen the stiff, rose-in-the-mouth caricature of ballroom tango, *The Tango Lesson* was a revelation. This was a different dance entirely — intimate, improvisational, grounded.

Tango teachers around the world reported a surge of new students who walked in saying, "I saw this film."
