# Finito Dies (1987)

> **HITM FLAG:** Sources on Finito are very scarce. The following is compiled from tango community mailing lists, blog posts, and oral history accounts. Exact dates, birth year, and cause of death could not be verified through authoritative sources.

In 1987, the Buenos Aires tango revival lost one of its brightest lights before it had barely begun. Ramón "Finito" Rivera (sometimes spelled Ribera) — widely considered the best social dancer of the 1980s revival — died at approximately age 55.

## Villa Urquiza Master

He was a master of the Villa Urquiza style, that elegant, walked, musical way of moving that defined the northern Buenos Aires milongas. Finito was part of the generation that had stopped dancing tango for 20 to 25 years during the decline and dictatorship, returning to the floor only when democracy made it possible again. He appeared in the film *Tango: Baile Nuestro* alongside Gerardo Portalea and Miguel Balmaceda, in a milonga scene that captured the authentic social style of tango de pista.

## A Loss Too Soon

His death at a relatively young age was a blow to a community that was just beginning to rediscover itself. The old milongueros were the living repositories of a dance tradition that had never been formally taught — you learned by watching, by dancing, by years of practice among men at the neighborhood clubs. Every one who died took irreplaceable knowledge with them.

Finito carried the legacy of the legendary "Milonguita," the dancer who never went on stage, and passed it forward for only a few brief years before it was cut short. His dancing can still be glimpsed in Ney Melo's documentary series *Origins of Villa Urquiza Style*.
