# UNESCO Heritage (September 30, 2009)

On September 30, 2009, in Abu Dhabi, a room of 400 cultural experts approved something that would have made the old compadritos laugh: tango was declared an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity by UNESCO. It was the first of 76 nominations examined that day, and it passed.

## Joint Nomination

The remarkable part was who submitted it — Argentina and Uruguay, jointly. The two nations had argued for decades over who "owned" tango, but for this, they set aside their rivalry and made a shared case. The recognition covered not just the dance but the entire cultural universe: the music, the poetry, the oral traditions, the social rituals of the milonga, the knowledge passed from milonguero to student in a close embrace.

## Second Attempt

It was tango's second attempt. In 2001, Argentina had tried alone and been rejected — UNESCO considered tango too "global and contemporary" to qualify as traditional heritage. The 2003 Convention for Safeguarding Intangible Cultural Heritage changed the rules, making transnational nominations possible.

## Acknowledgment of Origins

UNESCO's official description acknowledged tango's origins among "European immigrants, descendants of African slaves, and the natives known as criollos" in the Río de la Plata basin — a rare institutional acknowledgment of tango's multicultural roots. The designation committed both countries to developing preservation plans with UNESCO financial support, and gave tango communities in over 60 countries a new kind of legitimacy.

Tango wasn't just a dance; it was now officially part of the heritage of all humanity.
