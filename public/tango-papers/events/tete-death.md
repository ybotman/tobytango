# Tete Dies (January 7, 2010)

In the early hours of January 7, 2010 — two days before his 74th birthday — Pedro Alberto "Tete" Rusconi died in a boarding house at Pringles 84 in the barrio of Almagro, Buenos Aires. Just ten days earlier, he had been filmed dancing at Salón Canning, looking like he was floating.

## The Milonguero

Tete was *the* milonguero — the man whose dancing in close embrace made you understand that tango wasn't about steps but about listening. He started dancing at 14 in the milongas of Pompeya, learning the secrets of tango salón from a maestro known as "Tin." When the military dictatorship crushed tango culture in the 1970s, he stopped dancing for two decades. He came back in the 1980s revival, and it was as if he'd never left.

His famous instruction was simple: **"Dance the music. Because the music is the tango."**

## Pina Bausch

In 1995, the great German choreographer Pina Bausch saw Tete dance in Buenos Aires and invited him to Europe to perform in her piece *Nur Du* ("Only You") with Tanztheater Wuppertal. He danced it for three consecutive years. When someone asked Bausch why she chose this particular milonguero from all the dancers in Buenos Aires, she answered: **"He has an orchestra in his head."**

Tete reflected on this with characteristic humility: "She didn't mean I have an orchestra — she meant that I listen to the music well. Actually, that's the only thing I know how to do."

## Legacy

His approach to tango — taught through Susana Miller, who coined the term "milonguero style" based on his dancing — launched an entire global movement. Pina Bausch had died just months before him. The tango world lost Osvaldo Zotto the same week.

Tete's counsel to the community survived him as a kind of manifesto: **"I would like you to open your eyes to learn to dance; and to the teachers — try to bring the best dancers and teachers to teach tango as it ought to be taught."**
