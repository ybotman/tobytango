# Pepito Dies (April 29, 1996)

His real name was José Domingo Monteleone, and he came from Avellaneda — the working-class city across the Riachuelo from Buenos Aires. They called him Pepito Avellaneda, and he was the undisputed King of the Milonga.

## From Carnival to Legend

He started dancing at 12, at Carnival celebrations in his neighborhood. By 15, he was giving exhibitions at the Teatro Roma de Avellaneda. He danced *orillero* style — the tango of the outskirts, the poor neighborhoods — with a creativity and musicality that made people stop and watch. For milonga dancing specifically, with its staccato traspié and quick weight changes, no one could touch him.

He preferred D'Arienzo for the beat. **"Dancing is everything for me,"** he once said. **"I dance and I am nurtured. Milonga, for me, is my life."**

## The Pizzero

By day, Pepito was a pizzero — a pizza maker in his family's shop. Tango never paid his bills. In the 1990s, as the global revival accelerated, European dancers discovered him and begged him to teach.

He traveled to Amsterdam, where he taught at the Tango Alma school from 1991 to 1996. In June 1995, he crossed to the United States for the first time, teaching at the International Argentine Tango Congress at Northwestern University. He produced the instructional video series *Así se baila milonga*. He got a U.S. work visa good for five years.

He never got to use it.

## End

On April 29, 1996, Pepito died at 65, a man who had lived for dance in a world that had never figured out how to compensate him for his genius. The letters from European students kept arriving at an empty address.
