# 'Tango Argentino' Show (1983–1985)

In November 1983, as Argentina was shaking off the terror of military dictatorship, two Argentine set designers named Claudio Segovia and Héctor Orezzoli unveiled something radical at the Festival d'Automne in Paris: a show called *Tango Argentino* that violated every rule of commercial theater. There was a single set. No plot. The cast included aging, thick-waisted milongueros from Buenos Aires dancing alongside sleek professionals — all performing their own choreography.

The legendary partnership of Juan Carlos Copes and María Nieves headlined alongside couples like Virulazo and Elvira Santamaría, the Dinzels, and Nélida and Nelson Avila. Singer Roberto Goyeneche performed. The Sexteto Mayor played live. The Théâtre du Châtelet sold out every night, and on the final evening, crowds who couldn't get tickets stood outside the doors just to listen.

## Broadway

Two years later, on October 9, 1985, *Tango Argentino* opened on Broadway at the Mark Hellinger Theatre. It ran for 199 performances, earned a Tony nomination for Best Musical, and grossed over $6.2 million. Frank Sinatra came. Madonna came. Mikhail Baryshnikov came. Overnight, New York dance instructors reported demand for Argentine tango lessons tripling.

## Global Impact

The show toured the world for more than fifteen years and spawned imitators like *Forever Tango* and *Tango Pasión*. As choreographer Laura Falcoff later wrote, "If today you can find milongas in Verona or Alaska, the one responsible is *Tango Argentino*." It didn't just revive tango — it reintroduced the world to a dance it had almost forgotten existed.
