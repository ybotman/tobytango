# Gardel Dies (June 24, 1935)

At 2:51 on a Monday afternoon in Medellín, Colombia, the world lost the voice it never knew it couldn't live about. Carlos Gardel — "El Zorzal Criollo," "El Mago," the man who *was* tango — boarded a Ford Trimotor at Olaya Herrera Airport, bound for Cali. During takeoff, the overloaded aircraft swerved off the runway and slammed into a taxiing plane. The explosion killed 17 people, including Gardel, his lyricist Alfredo Le Pera, and two of his guitarists. He was 44 years old.

With Le Pera, he had written tangos that would become eternal — "Volver," "El Día Que Me Quieras," "Por Una Cabeza," "Mi Buenos Aires Querido" — and recorded more than 800 songs that transformed tango from a dance of the arrabal into a global art form. His Paramount films had carried tango to audiences across the Americas and Europe.

## The Mourning

The mourning was staggering. Gardel's body traveled through Colombia, Venezuela, New York City, and Rio de Janeiro before arriving in Montevideo, where it lay in state for two days. In Buenos Aires, tens of thousands lined the streets as his coffin was carried to La Chacarita Cemetery, where visitors still leave lit cigarettes at his statue.

## Legacy

Argentines say "Gardel sings better every day" — and they mean it. In 2003, UNESCO registered his voice in the Memory of the World program. He didn't just popularize tango; he gave it a soul that transcended borders, classes, and generations. His death froze him at the height of his powers, and in doing so, made him immortal.
