# Research Prompt: La Decadencia — The Decline of Argentine Tango (1955–1983)

**Document Type:** Historical White Paper for Tango History Timeline
**Target Audience:** Tango dancers and enthusiasts seeking deep historical understanding
**Cross-Reference Categories:** Argentina, Dancers & Couples, Orchestras, Europe, USA, Key Events
**Output Format:** Markdown with clear section headers, dates, names, and source-ready citations

---

## RESEARCH DIRECTIVE

Create a comprehensive white paper documenting **La Decadencia (The Decline) of Argentine Tango, 1955–1983**. This is tango's dark age — the nearly three-decade period when the dance nearly died. Understanding this era is essential for appreciating both what was lost and how remarkable the later revival was.

---

## SCOPE AND STRUCTURE

### I. THE FALL OF PERÓN AND IMMEDIATE AFTERMATH (1955–1960)

**Research these elements:**

1. **The Revolución Libertadora (September 16, 1955)**
   - The military coup that overthrew Perón
   - General Eduardo Lonardi, then General Pedro Eugenio Aramburu
   - Why the new regime viewed tango with suspicion (association with Peronism)
   - Immediate effects on nightlife, gatherings, curfews

2. **The Cultural Purge**
   - De-Peronization of Argentine culture
   - Tango as "music of the descamisados" — politically tainted
   - Which orchestras suffered? Which adapted?
   - Radio programming changes
   - Pugliese's repeated imprisonments (detail the timeline)

3. **Economic Changes**
   - End of Perón's labor protections
   - Impact on working-class leisure time and income
   - Musicians losing steady work
   - Clubs closing or converting to other uses

4. **Generational Shift Beginning**
   - Rock and roll arriving (Bill Haley, Elvis) — when exactly?
   - Young people rejecting parents' music
   - Tango as "old people's dance" begins

### II. THE LONG DECLINE (1960–1976)

#### A. Musical Evolution and Fragmentation

1. **Piazzolla's Departure**
   - Astor Piazzolla's nuevo tango (starting ~1955)
   - The split: concert music vs. dance music
   - Traditional milongueros' rejection of Piazzolla
   - "Music to listen to, not to dance to"
   - Piazzolla moving to Europe (timeline of his relocations)

2. **The Golden Age Orchestras in Decline**
   - Which orchestras continued? Which disbanded?
   - Death dates of major leaders:
     - Carlos Di Sarli (1960)
     - Juan D'Arienzo (continued until death 1976)
     - Aníbal Troilo (continued until death 1975)
     - Osvaldo Pugliese (continued, but struggled)
   - Quality of late recordings vs. Golden Age peak
   - Smaller ensembles, reduced budgets

3. **Recording Industry Changes**
   - Transition from 78 RPM to LP
   - Declining sales of tango records
   - Rise of tropical music, cumbia, folklore
   - Rock nacional emerging (late 1960s)

#### B. The Dance Nearly Dies

1. **The Milongas Close**
   - Which milongas survived? Which closed?
   - Neighborhood clubs converting to other purposes
   - The few remaining venues (where did people dance?)
   - Underground nature of remaining tango culture

2. **The Milongueros Stop Dancing**
   - Profile the generation that stopped:
     - Finito — when did he stop? When did he return?
     - Pupi Castello — his hiatus period
     - Carlos Perez — stopped dancing, worked as gravedigger
     - Tete Rusconi — what did he do during these years?
     - Osvaldo Cartery — his 30-year break
   - Why did they stop? (No places to dance? No partners? Social pressure?)
   - What happened to their skills during the hiatus?
   - Did any continue dancing through this period?

3. **Gender and Social Dynamics**
   - Women's role changing in Argentine society
   - The old códigos becoming irrelevant
   - Partnership patterns disrupted
   - The few who kept dancing — who were they?

#### C. Cultural Competition

1. **Rock Nacional**
   - Emergence of Argentine rock (Almendra, Sui Generis, Serú Girán)
   - Timeline: when did rock become dominant youth culture?
   - Rock clubs vs. milongas
   - Complete generational rupture in musical taste

2. **Folklore Revival**
   - Nueva canción movement
   - Mercedes Sosa, Atahualpa Yupanqui
   - Folk as politically acceptable alternative to tango

3. **Tropical and Cumbia**
   - Working-class dance alternatives
   - Cumbia villera emerging
   - Different social codes, different venues

4. **International Pop**
   - Disco era (1970s)
   - The twist, go-go
   - Tango as completely unfashionable

4. **The Music During Dictatorship**
