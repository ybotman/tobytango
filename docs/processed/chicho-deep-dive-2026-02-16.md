# Chicho Frúmboli: A comprehensive profile of tango's restless revolutionary

**Mariano "Chicho" Frúmboli is one of the three most influential living tango dancers**, a self-described "kamikaze" who helped shatter tango's formal vocabulary in the 1990s and has spent the decades since grappling publicly with the consequences. Born September 21, 1970, in Buenos Aires, he arrived at tango through rock drumming and theater, joined the legendary Naveira/Salas investigation group, and became the dancer Gustavo Naveira called "absolutely the best dancer I ever saw." Now in his mid-fifties and still touring intensively across Europe, he remains a polarizing and vital figure — a master improviser who rejects the "Tango Nuevo" label he helped create and advocates passionately for tango's essence over its spectacle.

## 1. Biography details

### Father and family

His father's given name does not appear in any publicly available source — he is referred to only as "my father" or "su padre" across all interviews. However, Chicho has spoken about him with specificity: **"My father had an artistic side that was significant. He was a fine arts professor, he studied the guitar, and I believe that this had a lot to do with my own artistic development, and creativity. I also remember that when I was a child my father often listened to Piazzolla, and that was my first contact with the tango; with the music more than anything."** (ATDRC interview, March 2008; source: brisbanehouseoftango.com.au/interview-with-mariano-chicho-frumboli/). The Spanish Wikipedia mirror confirms: "Su padre fue profesor de bellas artes y estudió guitarra." In the Gazzetta Tango interview, Chicho elaborated that the specific Piazzolla pieces his father played were "Adiós Nonino" and "Libertango," calling those memories "imborrables" (unforgettable) (source: gazzetta-tango.com/portfolio/entrevistita-a-chicho-frumboli/).

### Early life

Chicho's first encounter with tango was not in a class — it was at **Salón La Argentina**, an old Buenos Aires dance hall that no longer exists, where he went on a whim with a girlfriend. He described the scene: *"I went crazy with the faces, the bodies in movement as if they were glued to each other… the music."* But he did not pursue tango immediately. Two years passed before Victoria Vieyra, a classmate in his theater studies with Cristina Banegas, brought him back to tango through classes with Ricardo Barrios. He famously resisted at first: *"I was into rock-and-roll; I had long hair and played the drums. I hated tango, I didn't like it one bit, I couldn't even listen to it."* The pivot was physical: *"I embraced my dance partner for the first time and I got goose bumps. I said, 'there's something going on here…' and I never stopped."* (El Tangauta, December 2009; source: tangoforge.com/the-chicho-interview/).

At **age 13** (circa 1984), he received his first drum set and began formal music study. Wikipedia states he studied at the "David Lebón Music School" in Buenos Aires, though this detail carries a [clarification needed] tag and cannot be independently verified — David Lebón is a famous Argentine rock guitarist, not known as a school founder. Regardless, Chicho played drums for **14 years** in rock bands before tango overtook his life. From **1992 to 1998**, he simultaneously studied theater for eight years under the renowned actress and teacher Cristina Banegas.

His football team is **Huracán**. His favorite hobbies include making music on his PC and drawing. When asked about his first thought on waking, he replied: *"I think about the time I have left to live and that I mustn't waste it."* His future aspiration: *"A good father."* (El Tangauta, April 2005; source: tangoargentineric.wordpress.com).

### Current residence and status (2024–2025)

Chicho remains **based in Europe**, likely in France or Belgium, though no single source confirms an exact city as of 2025. After arriving in Europe in October 1998, he settled first in France and then in Belgium. His 2015 Facebook post describes Buenos Aires visits with "mixed feelings," suggesting he is not primarily based there. His 2024–2025 touring schedule — heavy with European festivals in London, Copenhagen, Zürich, Florence, Lyon, Mallorca, and Croatia — is consistent with a European home base. He and Juana Sepúlveda remain extremely active internationally.

### Awards and recognition

Tango has no formal awards system and Chicho does not participate in competitions. His most tangible recognition is through the film *Tango Libre*, which won the **Special Orizzonti Jury Prize at Venice** and received **10 nominations at the 4th Magritte Awards** (January 2014). Within the tango world, he is consistently listed alongside Juan Carlos Copes, Miguel Ángel Zotto, Sebastián Arce, Gustavo Naveira, and Pablo Verón as among the greatest tango dancers of all time (source: tangofilia.net). Naveira's quote — *"Chicho was absolutely the best dancer I ever saw"* — remains the most authoritative endorsement (source: tangoforge.com/gustavo-naveira-brian-dunn-2005-8/). He is described as one of "the big three" alongside Naveira and Salas (source: tango-amistoso.co.uk).

### Films and documentaries

### Teaching affiliations

Chicho has **not founded a formal tango school or academy**. In his 2015 Facebook post, he explicitly lamented: *"I regret that there was never an academy that brought together, at least in terms of the basic concepts, the key techniques."* He teaches primarily through international festival seminars and his annual **Tango Element Festival** in Baltimore (since 2009), organized through Eventbrite listings. In the early 2000s, he taught nuevo tango to theater artists in Paris for three years alongside Camilla Saracens. During COVID (2020–2021), he and Juana taught via video conference, described in the German journal *Bruchstücke* as demonstrating extraordinary methodical clarity.

He also produced **two instructional VHS videos** reviewed on tejastango.com: "Boleos and Ganchos" (90 min, with Brooke Burdett) and "Changes of Direction in Turns" (60 min, with Sharna Fabiano). Stephen Brown's review notes: *"Chicho is a one-time protégé of the modern master Gustavo Naveira... Chicho has developed his own reputation for his knowledge of tango's structure as well as for carrying some of Gustavo's ideas to extremes."*

## 3. Interviews and quotes — the full inventory

### Interview #1: El Tangauta, April 2005 (interviewer: Carlos Bevilacqua)

El Tangauta n°126. Available in French translation at tangoargentineric.wordpress.com.

Key quotes:

On discovering tango: *"In 1994, while I was studying theater, a classmate invited me to learn with her. I said no because I didn't like tango. I played the drums, it was more rock. But in the end, since she was getting annoyed, I accepted, I went with her and since then, I've never stopped dancing."*

On Tango Nuevo as marketing: *"It's a question I ask myself too… It's first and foremost a question of marketing… I feel close to the electronic orchestras in the way I dance. But beyond the fact that I know many musicians who play in those orchestras, I identify more with the music of Troilo, Pugliese and D'Arienzo."*

On improvisation: *"We choose the music 10 minutes before dancing, according to our mood and the atmosphere of the place… We improvise, we don't dance choreography. That, I got from Gustavo [Naveira]."*

On musicality: *"I am a musician, and watching dancers who don't dance in rhythm is something I cannot understand, any more than I can understand them dancing in a kind of bubble without the slightest life inside."*

On tango's essence: *"A very strong connection between the man and the woman who dance tango. Nowadays people learn two or three steps and go dancing… I really believe that dancing is thinking about how to embrace your partner, perceiving how she feels, how much she weighs, smelling her perfume, paying attention to how she takes your hand, if she trembles, if she sweats…"*

On being rejected: *"Of course I've been rejected. A few months ago, in a milonga in Paris, I invited a girl who was dancing with a contemporary style and she said: 'No, no thank you' and I walked away saying 'Ouch, that's tough!'"*

On his influence: *"Involuntarily, I think I changed the aesthetics of tango, from its image to its movements."*

### Interview #2: ATDRC, Buenos Aires, March 2008

Conducted by the Argentine Tango Dance Research Centre. Full English text at brisbanehouseoftango.com.au/interview-with-mariano-chicho-frumboli/.

**The 50/50 quote**: *"I don't think that woman is going to occupy more or less space, if not that the couple takes on more strength and power when it is a couple, with an equality between the two, and today that is really a division of 50/50. This has to do with the way the man is marking in the moment, if she cannot feel comfortable dancing, then I cannot dance."*

On subtle marking: *"If I want to take the movement to create a sacada, I have to communicate to my partner in the gentlest way… I can't mark only with my hands, I have to do a completely corporal marking, or I propose something and she responds but she does it with another proposal and I then follow her."*

On musicality as core identity: *"I believe that my best qualities have to do with my musicality, and obviously my creativity within that realm. I began as a musician and I continue to be one. The music is what moves me on all levels, I need to feel it in order to be able to dance."*

On his weakness: *"I believe my weakness has to do with my inhibition, I still don't feel that I have exploited and showed everything I have to show, almost as if I haven't been able to give myself completely as I would like to."*

On adapting to each partner: *"When I danced with Lucia I did things in one way, and when I began to dance with Eugenia she proposed a different way of dancing, and so my body had to adapt itself… the style is something you search for with your partner, and not something that you find separately."*

On tango's universality: *"My idea, and that of Gustavo Naveira and Fabian Salas as well, was always that the tango needed to be popular in the world. Obviously, it is born from one country and has its origins, but it needs to be universal. It cannot belong only to Buenos Aires."*

### Interview #3: El Tangauta #182, December 2009 (interviewer: Milena Plebs)

The landmark interview. Available in English translation at tangoforge.com/the-chicho-interview/ and tangochoseme.com; in French at tangoargentineric.wordpress.com. Extensively discussed at dance-forums.com/threads/35108/.

**The confession**: *"I couldn't really transmit what I learned. I was caught up in the madness of creativity, because I saw a new vein in the evolution of movement. I threw myself into it body and soul, and I lost the ability to transmit the essence of tango that I have inside me. For this reason, I think that these days there are many people who don't understand or don't know what the true essence of this dance is."*

**The contradictory role**: *"I know that my role is contradictory, because I also collaborated in the genesis of this new movement. At that time, I was tired of the strict milonguero codes that didn't correspond with my time and, in rebelling, I tried to make my way. Today, I have become a milonguero again (laughs); I am against people who don't do the cabeceo, who have no codes or respect."*

**The embrace as essence**: *"A few years ago I went to the 'La Trastienda' milonga organized by Horacio Godoy. I walked in and I saw you [Milena]. I wanted to dance with you but second-guessed myself… I remember we were talking, then we embraced each other and in that moment I felt 40 years of tango. In the embrace, do you understand? We hadn't taken a single step!… For me that was the most powerful moment of the tanda."*

On being a kamikaze: *"Maybe I'm a 'kamikaze.' It's what provokes in me a sensation or emotion that makes me move… What motivates me is being on the edge, the limit, on the point of falling, and managing to escape from that."*

On the milonga of Almagro: *"It took me five months to get on the dance floor of the milonga of Almagro, I didn't dare to, and I went every Sunday only to watch. One breathed an air of respect that cannot be found now."*

On music structure: *"I know the musical phrase and how long it lasts, I know when it ends, and I'll prepare the movement on the fly so that it corresponds perfectly to the music… [Understanding music structure] is the foundation of everything, crucial… There should be a deeper investigation at the level of the music itself. I'm not talking about rhythms, phrasing, or duration, but about structure, nuances and colors. There is so much richness to draw from and learn in the relationship with music. It's infinite!"*

On lost density: *"Before, people worked with precision and a particular aesthetic, in a functional and mechanical way that gave it a form, and a style. Making a movement or taking a step implied an expression of the entire body. Currently, not only has the essence been lost but the weight of the dance as well, its density and importance."*

### Interview #4: "Tengo una pregunta para vos" — 2xTango, October 2010, Buenos Aires

A 90-minute live Q&A event at Luna Llena Hotel, organized by Ute Neumaier. Chicho was the first guest in this series. The organizer became a fan after reading the 2009 El Tangauta interview. Referenced at soymilonguera.com. Video may no longer be publicly available.

### Interview #5: Gazzetta Tango (undated, in Spanish)

Full text at gazzetta-tango.com/portfolio/entrevistita-a-chicho-frumboli/. Conducted in Spanish.

On what exhausts him about tango: *"So many things… traditional tango, tango nuevo… milongas in some countries where they serve only water and fruit juices… hysterical women, showoff men… the business… the lack of creativity, the absence of new dancers with personality… the bent hand and the other one on women's asses… selective marathons, the 'famous' DJs! The enormous number of festivals everywhere… the lack of eagerness to learn… should I go on?"*

On the future: *"Tango today is installed… there's no going back, it's a machine that can't be stopped… The future today lies in education, understanding, respect and the love we owe this dance… Tango is a world full of its own life, difficult… the night, the drugs, the alcohol, the promiscuity… It's not just about doing a good colgada… it's about understanding the heart and where the soul of what we're dancing is."*

His top three tangos: **Como dos extraños, Quedémonos aquí, Pasional**. Top three orchestras: **Pugliese, Di Sarli, and "el gran Astor" (Piazzolla)**. Top three male dancers: **Gustavo Naveira, Julio Balmaceda, Osvaldo Soto**. Top three female dancers: **Milena Plebs, Guillermina Quiroga, Vanina Bilous**.

How to end a milonga: *"Chatting with no more than two real friends in a bar with a café con leche and medialunas… analyzing tango from every point of view… and believing we're solving all the quilombos that this thing we love gives us."*

### Statement #6: Facebook manifesto, August 2015

Widely translated and discussed. Full English text at tangowords.wordpress.com/2015/10/19/some-thoughts-from-chicho-frumboli/ and thetangolesson.wordpress.com/2015/12/23/some-thoughts-from-chicho-frumboli/.

Key excerpts: *"Buenos Aires always leaves me with mixed feelings… Sometimes it's pure love, at other times I hate it…"* He mourns the lost milongas of Almagro, Parakultural Centre in Chacabuco, Arlequines, and La Viruta "in its Golden Age" — *"a time when aesthetics weren't so important… a time when we were learning to dance, and we told ourselves that true authenticity lay in discovering our own dance."*

On the proliferation of teachers: *"There are countless 'maestros' and a ridiculous number of 'professionals', which is a double-edged sword."*

On competitions and style: *"It's difficult to understand why we have this 'dance factory' for competitions. What happened to tango…? To dancing? Having fun… seduction… playfulness… creativity…? Why teach styles? Because this limits our bodies, arms, backs, feet and necks. Why is it necessary to dress up as a 'tango professional'?… Why teach musicality? Why do they speak so much bullshit? Why aren't they really teaching people to dance?"*

The closing: *"Don't be cardboard cut-outs, guys. It's very cold and empty. You won't find tango by chasing a prize, or money, or fame. The Tango is within us… as it's always been."*

### Statement #7: Facebook post, November 2013

Discussed by Alberto Paz on elfirulete.wordpress.com/tag/chicho-frumboli/ under the title "Chicho is pissed off again." Paz frames it: *"After unintentionally inspiring an entire generation of dancers to act like circus acts on the dance floor, Chicho seems to have had some sort of epiphany."* Chicho wrote: *"I'm still learning… enjoying and wondering why those things are lost, why fashion and trends around the world are so empty?"* He criticized the rejection of Argentine DJs and professionals by certain European scenes and concluded by quoting a friend: *"The tango is stronger than all of that, the tango is above any trend, fashion and style."*

### Evolution of views — narrative arc

The chronological progression of Chicho's public statements traces a remarkable journey. In **2005**, he was energetic and revolutionary, already noting that Tango Nuevo was "a question of marketing" but still primarily an innovator. By the **2008** ATDRC interview, he articulated the 50/50 partnership philosophy in detail and placed each partner chronologically in his artistic development. The **2009** Milena Plebs interview marked the turning point — a public confession of responsibility for tango's loss of essence, declaring himself "a milonguero again." The **2015** manifesto was a full-throated critique of competition culture, style conformity, and the "dance factory." Through the **2020s**, he has maintained this traditionalist-revolutionary position while continuing to tour and teach globally.

### Tango Nuevo stance: EVOLVED AND CONSISTENT
His position has been remarkably consistent since at least 2005. He rejects "Tango Nuevo" as a commercial label applied by festival organizers, viewing tango's evolution as a continuous process dating back to Copes and Zotto. The 2008 formulation remains definitive: **"I don't think I am doing 'Tango Nuevo,' I feel that I am dancing tango."** What has evolved is the intensity of his critique. By 2009, he acknowledged personal responsibility for tango's loss of essence; by 2015, he was writing manifestos against competition culture and style conformity. He increasingly positions himself as a defender of tango's soul against commercialization — the revolutionary who became, in his own amused words, a "milonguero nuevo."

## Conclusion: What the existing record reveals and what remains hidden

This research uncovered substantial new material beyond the baseline: a complete inventory of **at least nine distinct interviews and public statements** spanning 2005–2021, the full details of his film career including the Venice-awarded *Tango Libre*, his first tango encounter at Salón La Argentina years before Victoria Vieyra's classes, his specific Piazzolla memories tied to "Adiós Nonino" and "Libertango," his preferred orchestras (Pugliese, Di Sarli, Piazzolla) and dancers (Naveira, Balmaceda, Soto), and a verified 2024–2025 touring schedule confirming he remains fully active and Europe-based.

Three notable gaps persist. **His father's name** appears nowhere in the public record — a deliberate privacy choice, perhaps, given how openly Chicho discusses his father's influence. **His exact current city of residence** is carefully unspecified; the evidence points to France or Belgium, but Chicho appears to live as a touring artist without broadcasting a fixed address. And **no text-form interviews from 2022–2025** were found, suggesting either a shift toward in-person engagement over published media or simply that recent interview content hasn't been indexed online. The Susanne Mühlhaus book in German likely contains additional unpublished insights but is behind a paywall. For a tango history project, the primary-source interviews from 2005, 2008, and 2009 remain the richest veins, with the 2015 Facebook manifesto serving as the most recent substantial statement of his evolving philosophy.
