## RESEARCH DIRECTIVE

Create a comprehensive white paper documenting **the great singers of the Golden Age (1935–1955)** — the cantores de orquesta who defined how tango was experienced.

**THE GAP BEING FILLED:** The current timeline framework has 11 orchestras indexed but only 1 singer (Gardel, who pre-dates the Golden Age). This is a significant gap. The cantor de orquesta was central to Golden Age tango — the relationship between singer and orchestra defined how dancers experience a tanda. D'Arienzo with Echagüe vs. D'Arienzo with Mauré are different musical worlds. The framework currently treats orchestras as monolithic entities when in reality they evolved through singer partnerships.

This paper fills that hole.

---

## WHY SINGERS MATTER TO DANCERS

Before diving into research, establish why singers matter:

1. **Different Singer = Different Dance**
   - The same orchestra with different singers creates completely different moods
   - Dancers choose tandas partly by singer, not just orchestra
   - A DJ's singer choices shape the emotional arc of a milonga

2. **The Singer-Orchestra Partnership**
   - Singers weren't interchangeable — they shaped the orchestra's sound
   - Some partnerships were legendary (Troilo-Fiorentino, D'Agostino-Vargas)
   - A singer's departure could transform an orchestra's character

3. **Emotional Vehicle**
   - The instrumental provides rhythm and walking
   - The singer provides emotional content
   - Lyrics matter — even when dancers don't understand Spanish
