## What This Is

We're building a comprehensive Tango History Timeline for tobytango.com — an educational resource for tango dancers who want to understand where this dance came from, how it evolved, and who the key figures were.

**Timeline spans 1880–2030 in 8 eras:**

#### Era: La Guardia Vieja (The Old Guard) — 1880–1920

#### Era: La Guardia Nueva (The New Guard) — 1920–1935
- **Status:** Partial content
- **Current summary:**
  - Tango becomes respectable and mainstream
  - Bandoneon becomes central instrument
  - First great orchestras form (De Caro, Firpo, Canaro)
  - Carlos Gardel becomes global voice of tango (dies 1935)
- **Key figures:** None listed yet
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: La Época de Oro (The Golden Age) — 1935–1955
- **Status:** POPULATED — comprehensive white paper exists
- **Current summary:**
  - 600 orquestas típicas formed; ~50 professional orchestras at peak
  - D'Arienzo's 1935 revolution returns tango to dance floors
  - The Big Four: D'Arienzo, Di Sarli, Troilo, Pugliese
  - ~50,000 recordings on 78 RPM shellac — the canon for today's milongas
  - Argentina becomes world's second-largest recorded music producer
- **Key figures:**
  - Juan D'Arienzo — El Rey del Compás, revived danceable tango
  - Carlos Di Sarli — El Señor del Tango, elegant walking music
  - Aníbal Troilo — Pichuco, perfect synthesis of rhythm and emotion
  - Osvaldo Pugliese — Dramatic, symphonic intensity
  - Rodolfo Biagi — Manos Brujas, staccato piano style
  - Ricardo Tanturi — Explosive combination with Alberto Castillo
  - El Turco José — Co-created Villa Urquiza style
  - Luis 'Milonguita' Lemos — Co-created Villa Urquiza style
- **Paper exists:** `/tango-papers/argentina/epoca-de-oro.md` (356 lines, comprehensive)

#### Era: La Decadencia (The Decline) — 1955–1983
- **Status:** Partial content
- **Current summary:**
  - Perón overthrown 1955, tango culture suppressed
  - Military governments impose curfews, view tango with suspicion
  - Rock and roll, folk music capture younger generations
  - Most milongueros stop dancing for 20-30 years
- **Key figures:** None listed yet
- **Paper planned:** Yes — prompt ready in outbox

#### Era: El Renacimiento (The Rebirth) — 1983–1995
- **Status:** POPULATED
- **Current summary:**
  - Democracy returns (1983), 'Tango Argentino' ignites global interest
  - Old milongueros return: Finito (1980), Pupi Castello (mid-1980s)
  - Susana Miller codifies 'milonguero style' (early 1990s)
  - Precious window where living Golden Age tradition can be learned
- **Key figures:**
  - Finito — Best social dancer of 1980s
  - Pupi Castello — El Maestro de los Maestros
  - Tete Rusconi — Close embrace master
  - Pepito Avellaneda — King of the Milonga
  - Ricardo Vidort — The Last Compadrito
  - Gerardo Portalea — Standard of Villa Urquiza
- **Paper exists:** pre-nuevo-masters.md (links to Dancers category)

#### Era: La Investigación (The Investigation / Early Nuevo) — 1995–2005

### Category 3: TANGO EUROPE (The European Journey)

**Timeline spans 1910–2030 in 10 eras:**

#### Era: First Tangomania (Paris Goes Mad) — 1910–1914
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Tango arrives in Paris 1910-1913, tangomania sweeps city, Vatican condemns it, Kaiser bans officers from dancing it, Europe gets tango before Argentina fully respected it
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: Interwar Period (European Golden Period) — 1920–1940
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Tango permanent fixture of European social dance, Paris retains some authenticity through Argentine expats, London develops stiff 'English tango', Finnish tango emerges as Nordic art form
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: The Disconnect (Lost Connection) — 1940–1980
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** WWII severs organic connection, European ballroom tango calcifies into competition formats, International Standard Tango: rigid, no improvisation, no connection, Paris remains one city where authentic thread persists
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: Piazzolla's Europe (Musical Survival) — 1970–1985
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Piazzolla keeps tango music alive in European consciousness, 'Libertango' (1974, Milan) becomes globally famous, Europeans know tango as concert music not social dance, musical and dance worlds almost completely separated
- **Key figures:** Astor Piazzolla
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: Paris Explosion ('Tango Argentino' Premiere) — 1983–1985
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** 'Tango Argentino' premieres Théâtre du Châtelet Nov 13 1983, Paris audiences stunned, transfers to Broadway 1985, demand for authentic instruction explodes
- **Key figures:** Claudio Segovia & Héctor Orezzoli (creators), Juan Carlos Copes & María Nieves (cast), Gloria & Rodolfo Dinzel (cast)
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: First Communities (Seeds Take Root) — 1985–1995
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Tiny communities form in Paris, Amsterdam, London, Berlin; dance enthusiasts who saw 'Tango Argentino' or traveled to BA; milonguero tradition largely unknown; Europeans dance open embrace, mixing ballroom habits
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: German & Nordic Wave (Northern Embrace) — 1990–2000
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Germany becomes one of tango's strongest European homes, Berlin Wall falls (1989) cultural openness, Pina Bausch invites Tete to Tanztheater Wuppertal (1994-1997), Finnish tango remains parallel universe
- **Key figures:** Pina Bausch (choreographer), Tete Rusconi (Bausch collaborator)
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: Film & Cultural Legitimacy (Permission Granted) — 1994–2002
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Bausch's work connects tango to European high-art, 'The Tango Lesson' (1997) Sally Potter/Pablo Verón, Carlos Saura's 'Tango' (1998), films give cultural permission
- **Key figures:** Sally Potter (director), Pablo Verón (film star)
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: Festival Era (The Great Migration) — 2000–2010
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** Festivals proliferate - nearly every weekend somewhere in Europe, Argentina's 2001-2002 crisis drives teachers to Europe, marathon and encuentro formats emerge as European innovations, Istanbul becomes major tango city
- **Paper planned:** Yes

#### Era: Maturation (Self-Sustaining Scenes) — 2009–2030
- **Status:** Partial
- **Summary:** UNESCO recognition (2009), most European cities have self-sustaining tango communities, neo-traditional turn mirrors global trend, European dancers travel to BA for social dancing not workshops
- **Paper planned:** Yes
