### The Big Four (need expanded profiles)

1. **Juan D'Arienzo** (1900-1976) — "El Rey del Compás"
   - Returned tango to 2/4 rhythm, staccato style
   - Key pianist: Rodolfo Biagi (1935-38)
   - Cabaret Chantecler residency
   - 4-decade RCA Victor contract
   - We have: Basic profile. Need: signature recordings by period, how his sound evolved, which singers for which eras

2. **Carlos Di Sarli** (1903-1960) — "El Señor del Tango"
   - Lost eye at 13, dark sunglasses trademark
   - Piano-led, "walking" music
   - RCA Victor period (1939-49): 155 sides
   - We have: Basic profile. Need: period breakdown (early/mid/late), singer partnerships, most essential recordings for dancers

3. **Aníbal Troilo** (1914-1975) — "Pichuco"
   - Marabú debut July 1, 1937
   - ~449 recordings
   - Nursery of talent (Piazzolla, Goñi, etc.)
   - We have: Good profile. Need: how his sound changed across decades, key recordings by period

4. **Osvaldo Pugliese** (1905-1995)
   - Communist, cooperative orchestra
   - "La Yumba" (1946) invented the yumba style
   - Red carnation tradition
   - We have: Good profile. Need: what makes his music challenging for dancers, signature pieces

## Research Scope

This paper should be **practical for dancers and DJs**, covering:

1. **Sound Signatures** — What makes each orchestra instantly recognizable? How would you describe the difference to a new dancer?

2. **Period Evolution** — How did each orchestra's sound change over time? What are the distinct periods?

3. **Singer Partnerships** — Which singers defined which eras? Key recordings with each?

4. **Essential Recordings** — For each orchestra, what are the 5-10 recordings every tango dancer should know?

5. **Dancing Character** — What type of dancing does each orchestra inspire? (elegant walking, playful rhythmic, dramatic pauses, etc.)

6. **Bandoneón & Piano** — Who were the key instrumentalists that defined each sound?
