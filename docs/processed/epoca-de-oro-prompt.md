### II. THE PEAK YEARS (1937–1950): Golden Age at Full Power

#### A. The Great Orchestras — Profile Each:

Research and provide detailed profiles including: formation date, key musicians, signature sound, most important recordings, peak years, and legacy.

1. **Juan D'Arienzo** (already active) — "El Rey del Compás"
   - The driving 2x4 rhythm
   - Key recordings: "La Cumparsita," "El Flete," "Pensalo Bien"
   - Succession of pianists: Biagi, Polito, Valdez

2. **Carlos Di Sarli** — "El Señor del Tango"
   - The elegant, melodic style
   - The "walking" quality beloved by dancers
   - Key recordings and singers (especially Roberto Rufino, Jorge Durán)

3. **Aníbal Troilo** — "Pichuco"
   - Debut at Marabú cabaret (July 1, 1937)
   - The balance of rhythm and emotion
   - Troilo as bandoneón virtuoso
   - Key singers: Francisco Fiorentino, Floreal Ruiz, Edmundo Rivero
   - The "musician's musician" — why other musicians revered him

4. **Osvaldo Pugliese**
   - Political dimensions (Communist Party membership, persecution)
   - The dramatic, orchestral style
   - La Yumba (1946) and its significance
   - Why dancers find Pugliese challenging and rewarding

5. **Ricardo Tanturi**
   - With singer Alberto Castillo — the explosive combination
   - "Así se Baila el Tango" as cultural artifact
   - The more accessible, rhythmic style

6. **Rodolfo Biagi** — "Manos Brujas" (Wizard Hands)
   - His departure from D'Arienzo
   - The staccato piano style
   - Distinctive rhythmic fingerprint

7. **Alfredo De Angelis**
   - The romantic style
   - Key singers: Carlos Dante, Julio Martel
   - Popularity with social dancers

8. **Miguel Caló**
   - The refined, elegant approach
   - Featured musicians who became leaders (Troilo, Francini, Pontier)
   - Key recordings with Raúl Berón

9. **Osvaldo Fresedo** — "El Pibe de la Paternal"
   - The aristocratic style
   - Earlier innovations (vibraphone, harp)
   - Bridge from Guardia Nueva to Golden Age

10. **Francisco Canaro**
    - Prolific recording output
    - Commercial success vs. artistic innovation debate
    - Role in tango's institutionalization

11. **Additional Orchestras to mention:**
    - Lucio Demare, Enrique Rodríguez, Ángel D'Agostino, Héctor Varela, Domingo Federico, Francini-Pontier, Alfredo Gobbi, Edgardo Donato

#### C. The Dance — How People Actually Danced

1. **Social Dance Culture**
   - No formal classes — learning by watching, practicing man-to-man
   - The "práctica" sessions in clubs
   - Gender dynamics: men proved themselves before dancing with women
   - The codes (códigos) emerging in milongas

2. **The Venues**
   - Confiterías: Ideal, La Richmond, Marzotto
   - Cabarets: Chantecler, Marabú, Tibidabo
   - Neighborhood clubs: The backbone of tango culture
   - Radio: How broadcasts expanded reach

3. **Emerging Regional Styles**
   - Centro style: Downtown confiterías, crowded floors, close embrace
   - Villa Urquiza style: Larger club floors, elegant walking, can open embrace
   - Orillero style: From the outskirts, more playful and improvisational
   - Canyengue influence: Still present in older dancers
